Sky-Blue Relief Vase with Dragon Handles

大清乾隆年製 — Dà Qīng Qiánlóng Nián Zhì · Imperial Monochrome Porcelain, Qianlong Period (1736–1795)

Sky-Blue Monochrome Glaze Qing Dynasty · Qianlong 1736–1795 Jingdezhen Imperial Kilns Relief Dragon & Cloud Decoration Underglaze Impressed Seal Mark Dronova GOM Method

Object Description

The vase belongs to the group of imperial monochrome porcelain of the Qianlong period (1736–1795), produced at the imperial kilns of Jingdezhen. The form is thin-walled, with a globular body flowing smoothly into a tall cylindrical neck; a pair of decorative handles is symmetrically placed on the neck. The proportions and precision of profile-shaping are consistent with wares from the palace workshops of the High Qing.

Beneath the transparent sky-blue glaze, relief decoration of dragons and clouds has been executed (comparable to the anhua / hidden-relief technique characteristic of Qianlong-period imperial monochrome porcelain). The modelling of the relief is soft, with a smooth transition into the body surface and a high degree of detail in the smaller elements; no traces of mechanical press-moulding were identified — the morphology of the relief points to hand-finishing of the model prior to glazing.

The glaze itself is transparent, glassy, sky-blue in tone with a slight turquoise undertone, with pronounced optical depth and a vitreous sheen. The thickness of the glaze layer is evenly distributed, with a soft, natural pooling within the recesses of the relief — an effect that forms during high-temperature firing and intensifies the colour in the deepest areas of the decoration.

Technological Interpretation

A sky-blue monochrome glaze of this type is formed through the complete fusion of the silicate glaze mass during high-temperature firing (above 1280 °C). The homogeneous vitreous matrix, high transparency, and absence of secondary coatings are characteristic of high-quality imperial production from Jingdezhen.

The soft, natural redistribution of glaze within the recesses of the underglaze relief is a technological feature that is difficult to reproduce artificially: it arises as a side effect of the more fluid glaze, at peak firing temperature, flowing into the lower areas of the decoration, creating a local intensification of tone precisely where the relief is deeper. This is consistent with the pattern observed on the present vessel.

The slight glaze run toward the base and the mild darkening of the unglazed footring after firing are likewise expected technological features for this group of wares.

Diagnostic Checklist

  • Globular body with a smooth transition into a tall cylindrical neck, symmetrical paired handles
  • Thin-walled porcelain body, proportional imperial form
  • Relief decoration of dragons and clouds with soft modelling, no traces of mechanical press-moulding
  • Transparent, vitreous sky-blue glaze with turquoise undertone and high optical depth
  • Natural pooling of glaze within relief recesses, consistent with high-temperature firing
  • Unglazed footring of pale, dense body material with edge darkening
  • Underglaze relief six-character seal mark Da Qing Qianlong Nian Zhi
The overall set of macroscopic features (form, relief decoration, character of the glaze, footring treatment, mark morphology) is consistent with the technology of Qianlong-period (1736–1795) imperial monochrome porcelain and with the group of wares represented in the collections of The Palace Museum (Beijing) and the National Palace Museum (Taipei).
