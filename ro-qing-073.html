Double-Gourd Vase with Yellow Sgraffito Ground and Famille-Rose Medallions

大清乾隆年制 — Dà Qīng Qiánlóng Nián Zhì · Yellow-Ground Sgraffito (Kaiguang) Porcelain with Famille-Rose Enamels, Qianlong Mark and Period (1736–1795)

Yellow Sgraffito Ground Qing Dynasty · Qianlong 1736–1795 Jingdezhen Kilns (attributed) Double-Gourd (Hulu) Form Overglaze Iron-Red Seal Mark Optical Microscopy Observations

Object Description

A double-gourd (hulu) form vase, its two lobes covered in a continuous yellow enamel ground worked in the sgraffito technique — a fine scrolling diaper pattern incised into the body before the enamel was applied, so that the pattern remains visible as a low-relief texture beneath the transparent yellow. Set against this ground are famille-rose medallions of lotus and floral scrollwork, and, on the upper lobe, a single character in overglaze iron red identified in the accompanying material as "shou" (寿, longevity).

Detail of a lotus medallion beside the iron-red "shou" character, and the base showing a turquoise-glazed interior/footwell with a seal mark.

The floral medallions are painted in layered famille-rose enamels with soft internal shading from petal centre to edge, and fine grey-black outlining; brushwork varies slightly from motif to motif rather than repeating identically, consistent with hand painting rather than a printed or stencilled pattern. The sgraffito ground itself shows the incised scrollwork running continuously across the join between the two lobes of the vase, with the transparent yellow enamel pooling slightly within the engraved lines.

The base carries a turquoise glaze around a seal mark rendered in overglaze iron red (rather than the underglaze cobalt blue described in the accompanying notes for this mark type) — this is a discrepancy worth recording rather than resolving in favour of either description, since it affects how the mark should be read. As with any reign mark, its reading is considered here alongside the vessel's form, glazes and enamels rather than as an isolated proof of date.

Optical Microscopy

The observations below concern the yellow sgraffito ground and the turquoise glaze, examined by optical microscopy. This section is limited to optical features; the elemental composition of the enamels and glazes is not addressed here.

Yellow Sgraffito Ground — Boundary with Adjacent Enamel

Instrument Unconfirmed

At magnification, the incised diaper lines beneath the yellow enamel appear as shallow, slightly irregular grooves with a glassy pooled surface where the transparent enamel has settled into them; the transition toward the adjacent enamel field is gradual rather than a hard printed edge. The image was obtained at a magnification whose device and exact power are not documented, which limits the precision of interpretation; the observation is presented as preliminary.

Turquoise Enamel (Interior and Base)

Instrument Unconfirmed

The turquoise enamel surface shows fine, irregularly distributed reflective inclusions and a faint fracture-like surface texture at this magnification. As with the observation above, the imaging device and exact magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary and supplementary to direct examination.

This section is limited to optical observation of the yellow sgraffito ground and turquoise enamel. The instrument and magnification used for the images above are not documented, and this is noted accordingly.
