Double-Gourd Vase with Relief Flowers and Birds on Celadon and Red Ground

Double-gourd (hulu) form vase with tall neck and two globular lobes; sage-green ground with scrolling floral arabesques in red, blue, yellow, and white; high-relief cartouches on a red ground with gilt-highlighted flowers and long-tailed birds; a gilt meander band at the waist; dark blue-green glazed base with polychrome scrollwork and a small raised gilt square mark.

Double-Gourd (Hulu) Form High-Relief Decoration Celadon-Toned Ground Gilt Highlighting Qing Dynasty

Object Description

The vase has a double-gourd (hulu) silhouette: a tall neck with a gilt floral band at the rim, an upper globular lobe, and a larger lower globular lobe, separated by a waisted section bordered with a gilt meander (key-fret) band. The ground on both lobes is a sage/olive green enamel, covered overall with scrolling floral arabesques in red, blue, yellow, purple, and white.

Set into the green ground are high-relief cartouches with a bright red interior field, each containing a raised, gilt-highlighted composition of peonies and other blooms together with a long-tailed bird in flight or perched among foliage. The birds are rendered with raised, textured gilt plumage; their pose and setting are broadly consistent with a phoenix (fenghuang) or a pheasant, but the photographs do not allow a more specific identification.

The base is covered in a dark blue-green (teal) glaze, decorated with a dense polychrome scrollwork of red, purple, yellow, and brown motifs. A small, raised, gold-toned square mark sits at the centre of the base, bearing a compact arrangement of characters.

Note
At the resolution supplied, the mark's individual characters are not clearly legible as readable script. The accompanying documentation does not provide a specific reading for this mark either; it is described here only as a raised, gilt, square seal-form mark of undetermined text, considered together with the vessel's form, ground, and decoration rather than as a proof of date.
Scope Note
The accompanying documentation includes an "Attribution" section (period, kiln, and function) and an "Expert Commentary" affirming imperial court production, along with compositional claims about the gold and pigments (e.g. "genuine gold leaf," "natural patina," "high-fired copper pigments," multiple firings evidenced by micro-fissures, "no evidence of transfer printing"). Elemental/material composition analysis and authentication conclusions fall outside the scope of this catalogue entry, which is limited to what is directly observable in the supplied photographs and to the optical microscopy observations below. The documentation's stated height (50 cm) could not be independently checked, as no photograph includes a scale reference.

Optical Microscopy

The observations below concern a gilt relief surface, a pale blue-toned area, and two glaze boundary areas, examined by optical/macro photography. This section is limited to optical features; the elemental composition of the pigments, glaze, and gilding is not addressed here.

Gilt Relief Surface

Instrument Unconfirmed

The gilt surface shows a granular, faceted texture with distinct raised and recessed zones, the recesses appearing darker and less reflective than the raised areas. Fine hairline marks cross parts of the surface. The imaging device and exact magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary and limited to optical appearance.

Pale Blue-Toned Area

Instrument Unconfirmed

The image is soft and low in contrast, showing a pale blue field with faint reddish specks and a bright diagonal reflection; a fine network of light lines is visible but not sharply resolved. Given the blur, only a general colour and reflectivity impression can be reported rather than a specific microstructural observation. The imaging device and magnification are not documented.

Blue / Green Glaze Boundary

Instrument Unconfirmed

A deep blue, granular zone with fine bright specks meets a paler, tan-to-green granular zone along a fairly distinct, gently curving boundary; a bright specular highlight runs along the boundary line. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Dark / Green Glaze Boundary

Instrument Unconfirmed

A near-black, granular zone meets a pale green, similarly granular zone along a sharp, irregular boundary; a fine hairline crack crosses the dark zone and continues faintly into the paler area. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary and limited to optical appearance.
