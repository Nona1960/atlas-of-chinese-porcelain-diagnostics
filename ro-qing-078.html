Large Vase with Pink Sgraffito Neck, Blue Band and Famille-Rose Pheasant Panel

乾隆年制 — Qiánlóng Nián Zhì · Famille-Rose (Fencai) Porcelain with Sgraffito Neck Ground, Qianlong Mark and Period (1736–1795)

Pink Sgraffito Neck Ground Qing Dynasty · Qianlong 1736–1795 Jingdezhen Kilns (attributed) Famille-Rose Bird Panel Underglaze-Blue Mark on Turquoise Base Optical Microscopy Observations

Object Description

A large ovoid vase with a widening neck covered in a dense pink enamel ground worked in the sgraffito technique, over which a scrolling floral pattern with lotus-type blossoms is painted; the neck ground is bordered at the rim by a narrow gilt band. Below the neck, a solid blue band carries a low-relief scrolling texture and a pale rosette medallion, meeting a large shaped white-ground panel on the body that is painted in famille-rose enamels with a long-tailed pheasant, several small songbirds on a flowering branch, peonies, grasses and rockwork.

Detail of the neck ground and its transition to the blue band; and the base, covered in a turquoise glaze with a square underglaze mark.

The pheasant and songbirds are painted with graduated shading in the plumage and fine individual feather strokes rather than a flat, repeating pattern, and the peony petals show soft internal colour transitions typical of layered famille-rose enamel work. The blue band beneath the neck shows a fine, low-relief scrolling texture under the glaze rather than a sharply contrasting painted overlay; the accompanying documentation describes this band as carrying an elaborate lace-like white surface ornament, which is not clearly resolved as such in the photographs available — what is visible is a subtler relief texture within the blue enamel itself, and this difference is noted rather than resolved.

The base is covered in a turquoise glaze and carries a square underglaze-blue mark reading "Qianlong Nian Zhi" (乾隆年制), consistent in form with the four-character mark type documented elsewhere in this atlas. As with any mark, it is considered here alongside the vessel's form, ground and painting rather than as an isolated proof of date.

Optical Microscopy

The observations below concern the pink sgraffito ground, the blue band, a gilt surface, and a white glazed surface, examined by optical microscopy. This section is limited to optical features; the elemental composition of the pigments and glazes is not addressed here.

Pink Sgraffito Ground

Instrument Unconfirmed

The pink enamel shows a fine granular surface with low, evenly curved relief ridges beneath it, consistent with an incised pattern covered by enamel, meeting a white glazed area along a dark, slightly irregular boundary line. The imaging device and exact magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Blue Band

Instrument Unconfirmed

A diagonal band of denser blue pigment, with small darker concentrations, crosses a paler blue ground; the transition between the two tones is gradual rather than sharply defined. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Gilt Surface

Instrument Unconfirmed

The gilt area shows a directional, granular texture with strong reflective highlights and areas of shadow, consistent with a metallic surface catching light unevenly across a worn or textured gilt layer. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

White Glazed Surface

Instrument Unconfirmed

The white glazed surface shows scattered fine dark and light-brown inclusions distributed across an otherwise even, slightly granular ground. The specific decorative element photographed is not confirmed, and it is presented here as a general glaze-surface observation; the imaging device and magnification are not documented.

This section is limited to optical observation of the surfaces shown above. The instrument and magnification used for these images are not documented, and this is noted accordingly.
