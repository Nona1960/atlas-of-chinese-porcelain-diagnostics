Catalogue No. RO-QING-080

Yellow-and-Turquoise Ground Vase with Puce-Rose Landscape Cartouches

Broad-shouldered vase with flaring trumpet neck, alternating yellow and turquoise enamel grounds with scrolling floral ornament, framed cartouches painted in a monochrome puce-rose (purplish-rose) enamel depicting pavilion landscapes, three applied and glazed maroon relief bosses at the shoulder, and a small underglaze-blue seal-form mark on the turquoise-glazed base.

Famille Rose / Fencai / Yangcai Puce-Rose Monochrome Cartouches Yellow & Turquoise Grounds Applied Relief Bosses Underglaze-Blue Base Mark Qing Dynasty

Object Description

The vase has a broad, rounded body rising to a short waisted neck that flares into a wide trumpet-form mouth, standing on a spreading foot. The neck is decorated with a yellow enamel ground bearing scrolling floral vinework, interrupted by a large white-reserved cartouche painted in a soft purplish-rose (puce) monochrome enamel with a lakeside pavilion, trees, and distant hills, rendered in a manner recalling ink-wash landscape painting. A narrow band of blue lotus scrollwork on yellow separates the neck from the shoulder.

The body carries an alternating scheme of turquoise and yellow enamel grounds, both covered with fine scrolling floral vinework in white, green, and iron-red outline. Set into the turquoise ground on the lower body is a further white-reserved cartouche with a puce-rose landscape of low mountains and a body of water, echoing the neck panel's palette and technique.

Three raised, glazed maroon/copper-red bosses are applied at the shoulder — two flanking the sides where the neck meets the body, and one larger roundel centred on the front. Each carries a molded relief design picked out with thin gilt line-work.

Note
The accompanying documentation describes these as "lion-mask handles." In the supplied photographs, the bosses read as raised, rounded medallions with molded relief and gilt outlining; individual leonine features — eyes, mane, muzzle — are not clearly resolved at the available resolution and angle. The bosses are described here as seen, without adopting the "lion-mask" identification as confirmed.

The footrim area and the visible portions of the base are covered in a turquoise glaze. A small squarish seal-form mark appears on the turquoise-glazed base in underglaze blue, compact and set within a narrow rectangular border.

Note
The accompanying documentation reads this as a "four-character Yu zhi (御制)" mark. At the resolution supplied, the individual strokes of the mark are not clearly legible enough to independently confirm a character count, and it is worth noting that 御制 conventionally comprises two characters rather than four. The mark is recorded here per the accompanying documentation, alongside this open discrepancy, and — as with any mark — it is considered together with the vessel's form, ground, and painting rather than as an isolated proof of date.

Optical Microscopy

The observations below concern the puce-rose enamel of the landscape cartouches and a gilt surface, examined by optical/macro photography. This section is limited to optical features; the elemental composition of the pigments and glazes is not addressed here.

Puce-Rose Enamel, Landscape Cartouche

Instrument Unconfirmed

A deep magenta-purple zone diffuses gradually into paler pink-white areas, with a soft, feathered rather than hard-edged boundary between the two tones, and scattered fine dark points within the darker zone. The imaging device and exact magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary and limited to optical appearance.

Gilt Surface, Applied Boss

Instrument Unconfirmed

The gilt area shows a directional, granular texture with fine flake-like particulate patterning and strong reflective highlights alternating with shadowed regions, consistent with a metallic surface catching light unevenly across a textured gilt layer; a reddish-brown ground is visible at the lower edge of the frame. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary and limited to optical appearance.
