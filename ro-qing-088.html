Catalogue No. RO-QING-088

Pierced Double-Walled Vase with Red Ground and Famille Rose Landscape Medallions

Double-walled vase with a rounded body and tall flared neck; a luminous red ground on the neck and shoulder with incised scrolling florals in turquoise, yellow, blue, and white; a pierced (reticulated) outer wall on the body enclosing oval medallions painted with river-and-mountain landscapes; turquoise-glazed neck interior and base; a small seal-form mark in underglaze blue on the base.

Double-Walled Openwork Red Yangcai Ground Famille Rose Landscape Medallions Underglaze-Blue Base Mark Qing Dynasty

Object Description

The vase rises from a gently spreading foot to a rounded body and a tall, flared neck. The neck and shoulder are covered in a saturated red ground, beneath which a dense pattern of fine incised spirals underlies scrolling floral sprays painted in turquoise, yellow, blue, and white enamels. A scalloped valance border of stylised cloud-form swags in yellow and turquoise, each centred on a small flower, separates the red neck zone from the body below.

The body is formed as a pierced, reticulated outer wall in a pale, unglazed-toned porcelain, cut in an interlocking geometric fretwork pattern. Set into this openwork is an oval medallion reserving a painted river-and-mountain landscape with a pavilion, trees, and a distant boat, rendered in soft famille rose enamels, accompanied by a brush-written inscription and a small red seal.

Looking down into the mouth, the neck interior is glazed turquoise, narrowing toward a dark recess where a further glazed rim is visible, consistent with an inner liner behind the pierced outer wall (i.e., a genuinely double-walled construction).

Note
You have described the inner vessel as painted with cobalt-blue decoration. The supplied interior photograph is quite dark within the recess, and no clearly resolved blue painted design is visible in it — only a glazed rim and a shadowed opening. This entry records the presence of an inner wall/liner as visible, but the cobalt-blue decoration on it is noted per your description rather than independently confirmed from this photograph; a better-lit photo of the inner liner would allow that to be added with confidence.

The base is glazed turquoise and bears a small, rectangular seal-form mark in underglaze blue at its centre.

Note
The accompanying documentation reads this as a four-character Qianlong seal mark. At the resolution supplied, the mark's individual strokes are not fully resolved, and the character count could not be independently confirmed from the photograph. The mark is recorded here per the accompanying documentation, and — as with any mark — it is considered together with the vessel's form and decoration rather than as an isolated proof of date.

The accompanying documentation notes a comparable pierced vase with famille rose panels and a purplish-pink (rather than red) ground in the National Palace Museum, Taipei (documentation cites inv. no. 69, dated 1743). This is recorded here as the documentation's own comparative reference; it has not been independently verified against museum records and is not treated as establishing the present object's date or origin.

Optical Microscopy

The observation below concerns the red ground enamel, examined by optical/macro photography. This section is limited to optical features; the elemental composition of the pigment and glaze is not addressed here.

Red Ground Enamel

Instrument Unconfirmed

A deep red enamel surface is crossed by a network of fine, curving crackle lines with a slightly raised, glossy appearance relative to the surrounding matte-red field, along with a scattering of small dark specks. The imaging device and exact magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary and limited to optical appearance.
