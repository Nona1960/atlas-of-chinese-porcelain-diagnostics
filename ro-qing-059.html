Pale-Blue Celadon Vase with Deer-Head Handles

大清乾隆年製 — Dà Qīng Qiánlóng Nián Zhì · Imperial Monochrome Celadon Porcelain, Qianlong Period (1736–1795)

Tianlan / Clair-de-Lune Glaze Qing Dynasty · Qianlong 1736–1795 Jingdezhen Imperial Kilns Archaistic Form & Deer-Head Handles Underglaze-Blue Seal Mark Dronova GOM Method

Object Description

The examined vase belongs to the group of imperial monochrome celadon porcelains produced at the Imperial kilns of Jingdezhen during the Qianlong reign (1736–1795). The vessel combines an archaistic bronze-inspired form — an ovoid body with broad, rounded shoulders, a gently tapering cylindrical neck, a thickened everted rim, and a low circular foot — with a translucent pale-blue tianlan glaze, relief decoration beneath the glaze, and a pair of applied deer-head handles. The thin-walled, high-density porcelain body and the balanced proportions are consistent with imperial display vessels reviving ancient bronze ritual forms, a device central to the artistic programme of the Qianlong court.

The decorative programme, formed in the body before glazing, includes finely carved upright banana leaves on the neck, a precisely executed leiwen (key-fret) border, a continuous ruyi cloud collar, low-relief auspicious medallions, and sharply defined upright lappets above the foot. The relief retains crisp definition while appearing optically softened beneath the transparent glaze — consistent with the glaze having been applied over, and fired after, the completed relief carving, rather than the decoration being added on top of an already-glazed surface.

The monochrome glaze itself is the principal diagnostic feature of the piece: a translucent, pale-blue colour with a soft, jade-like optical depth, a homogeneous vitreous surface, and a strong glossy reflection. Colour intensity varies across the surface — a natural pooling within the recessed decoration and a lighter appearance over raised relief — an effect produced by differences in glaze thickness rather than by any applied pigment, and characteristic of the optical modelling seen on imperial celadon glazes of this type.

Technological Interpretation

The interaction between glaze and relief is one of the principal diagnostic criteria for this type of monochrome celadon: increased glaze thickness within the recessed ornament and a thinner glaze layer over the raised details, joined by a smooth, uninterrupted transition between relief and surrounding surface. This differential thickness enhances the optical depth of the glaze and is characteristic of imperial monochrome porcelain manufactured at Jingdezhen during the eighteenth century, where colour variation follows the topography of the underlying relief rather than any separately applied decoration.

The deer-head handles were attached to the body before glazing and fired as an integral part of the vessel: the modelling is individual rather than mould-repeated in identical form, the junction between handle and wall is seamless, and glaze coverage is continuous across the attachment area, with no visible assembly defects or secondary adhesive traces.

The overall glaze behaviour — a homogeneous vitreous surface with strong glossy reflection, combined with the described thickness-driven colour modelling — is consistent with a controlled high-temperature reduction firing typical of imperial tianlan (clair-de-lune) celadon production at Jingdezhen during the High Qing period.

Gemmological Optical Microscopy

Three magnified images of the glaze surface were supplied for this object.

Zone A — Glaze Pooling Near a Handle Attachment

Close-up

A diagonal band of pale olive-buff discoloration with scattered dark speckled inclusions crosses a smoother, pale bluish-grey glazed field; a bright linear highlight indicates a curved, glossy surface. The discoloured band is consistent with a firing-related tonal variation or an area of glaze accumulation near a raised or attached element, rather than an applied surface stain.

Zone B — Glaze / Body Interface

Close-up

A pale teal-grey glassy field with a soft, slightly mottled internal texture and a cluster of brighter, granular reflections toward the right of frame, consistent with a zone of denser micro-texture or light scattering within the glaze body. The overall impression is of a continuous vitreous layer rather than a layered or applied coating, though.

Diagnostic Checklist

  • Archaistic, bronze-inspired ovoid body with tapering neck and low circular foot
  • Crisply carved subglaze relief — key-fret border, ruyi collar, medallions, upright lappets
  • Translucent pale-blue tianlan (clair-de-lune) glaze with soft, jade-like optical depth
  • Natural colour pooling in recessed ornament, lighter tone over raised relief
  • Individually modelled deer-head handles with seamless attachment and continuous glaze coverage
  • Neatly trimmed footring, dense white body, pale buff firing tone, fully glazed base
  • Underglaze-blue six-character seal mark Da Qing Qianlong Nian Zhi in precise seal script
The macroscopic evidence — the archaistic bronze-form silhouette, the crisply carved and glaze-integrated relief programme, the translucent tianlan glaze with thickness-driven colour modelling, the individually modelled deer-head handles, and the morphology of the underglaze-blue reign mark — is consistent with imperial monochrome celadon porcelain produced at the Imperial kilns of Jingdezhen during the Qianlong period. The third frame is too unfocused to contribute further evidence.
