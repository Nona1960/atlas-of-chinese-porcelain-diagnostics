Catalogue No. RO-QING-087

Cobalt-Blue Ground Vase with Gilt Relief and Red Enamel Panels

Baluster vase with articulated neck and undulating rim; deep cobalt-blue ground engraved overall with gilt line-work of carp, chrysanthemums, and cloud scrolls; mei-hua-shaped red enamel reserves with raised gilt relief of a long-tailed bird among peonies and a flowering tree; two spiral gilt handles at the neck; pale glazed base with a small raised seal-form mark.

Cobalt-Blue Ground Gilt Engraved Decoration Red Enamel Relief Panels Spiral Gilt Handles Qing Dynasty

Object Description

The vase has a baluster body with an articulated neck rising to a flared, undulating (scalloped) mouth rim. Two gilt handles in a spiral, coiled loop form are applied at the neck-to-shoulder junction.

Note
The accompanying documentation describes these as "twin dragon handles with spiral bodies." In the supplied photograph, the handles read as coiled gilt spirals/loops; individual dragon features — head, eyes, scales — are not clearly resolved at the available resolution and angle. The handles are described here as seen, without adopting the "dragon" identification as confirmed.

The body is covered overall in a deep cobalt-blue ground, densely engraved with fine gilt line-work depicting a carp (koi) amid water plants, small chrysanthemum blossoms, and stylised cloud-form motifs. Set into the ground is a large, mei-hua (plum-blossom) shaped reserve with a red enamel field, bearing a raised gilt relief composition of a long-tailed bird — consistent with a peacock or similar bird, per the accompanying documentation, though the photograph does not allow a more specific identification — perched near a flowering tree and peonies, all rendered in low relief and gilded.

Above the foot, a band of black-ground gilt lappets separates the body from the base. The base itself is glazed pale (celadon-toned), set within a slightly recessed foot, and bears a small, raised, square mark that appears gilded, at the centre.

Note
The accompanying documentation reads this as a raised six-character reign mark, 大清乾隆年製. At the resolution supplied, the mark's individual strokes are not clearly legible, and a specific character count could not be independently confirmed from the photographs. The mark is recorded here per the accompanying documentation, and — as with any mark — it is considered together with the vessel's form and decoration rather than as an isolated proof of date.
Scope Note
The accompanying documentation includes compositional claims about the gilding and enamel (e.g. "genuine gold leaf application confirmed," an "iron-based enamel system," partial oxidation of the metallic layer "consistent with age") and a Conclusion affirming attribution to "imperial or court-related production contexts." Elemental/material composition analysis and authentication conclusions fall outside the scope of this catalogue entry, which is limited to what is directly observable in the supplied photographs and to the optical microscopy observations below. The documentation also notes the vase was acquired at auction in 2014 with limited seller-provided provenance; this is recorded as stated but not independently verified.

Optical Microscopy

The observations below concern the raised mark, a dark glaze area, a pink/red enamel area, and the boundary between the cobalt glaze and the unglazed body, examined by optical/macro photography. This section is limited to optical features; the elemental composition of the pigments, glaze, and gilding is not addressed here.

Raised Mark, Macro Detail

Instrument Unconfirmed

The image shows a raised, roughly rectangular relief form with a granular, tan-to-gilt surface texture, against a paler surrounding glaze. The image is soft/out of focus at this magnification, which limits resolution of individual strokes within the mark. The imaging device and exact magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Dark Glaze Area

Instrument Unconfirmed

A dark, near-black granular zone occupies most of the frame, meeting a lighter, granular tan zone along a diagonal boundary in the upper right. Scattered fine bright specks are visible within the dark zone. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary and limited to optical appearance.

Pink/Red Enamel Area

Instrument Unconfirmed

A salmon-pink enamel surface shows a fine, even granular texture with scattered small dark pits and a few larger, irregular dark specks. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary and limited to optical appearance.

Cobalt Glaze / Unglazed Body Boundary

Instrument Unconfirmed

A deep cobalt-blue glazed zone meets a pale, granular, unglazed (biscuit) zone along a soft, gradually diffusing boundary rather than a hard edge. Fine dark speckling is visible within the unglazed area, consistent with an unglazed porcelain surface. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.
