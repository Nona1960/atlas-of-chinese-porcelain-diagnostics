Large Vase with Yellow Ground, Archaistic Scroll Handles and Famille-Rose Floral Panel

乾隆年制 — Qiánlóng Nián Zhì · Famille-Rose (Fencai) Porcelain with Yellow Scrollwork Ground, Qianlong Mark and Period (1736–1795)

Imperial Yellow Ground Qing Dynasty · Qianlong 1736–1795 Jingdezhen Kilns (attributed) Pierced Archaistic Scroll Handles Underglaze-Blue Mark on Turquoise Base Optical Microscopy Observations

Object Description

A large vase with a widening neck covered in a dense imperial-yellow ground, over which a fine scrolling lotus pattern is painted in famille-rose enamels, bordered above and below by narrower bands of pink and floral scrollwork. The main body carries a white-ground panel painted with chrysanthemums, peonies, rockwork and foliage, meeting a narrow pink border band above the foot.

Detail of the border band and rockwork; and the base, covered in a pale turquoise glaze with a square underglaze-blue mark.

A pair of pierced, scroll-form handles in turquoise-green and iron-red enamel is applied at the neck. The accompanying documentation describes these as dragon-form handles with fine scale modelling; as photographed, the handles show an abstract hook-and-cloud scroll outline, and no clearly discernible dragon head, claws or scale detail can be confirmed at this resolution — this is recorded as an open point rather than affirmed either way.

Within the yellow ground, the lotus scrollwork is drawn with fine, individually varying line-work rather than a mechanically repeating unit, and the border band combines pink, yellow and blue floral motifs with soft internal shading in the petals, consistent with layered famille-rose enamel painting.

The base is covered in a pale turquoise glaze and carries a square mark in underglaze blue arranged as four characters reading "Qianlong Nian Zhi" (乾隆年制). The accompanying documentation describes this as a six-character mark reading "Da Qing Qianlong Nian Zhi" (大清乾隆年制); as photographed, only four characters are visible, without the "Da Qing" (大清) prefix, and this discrepancy between the description and the photograph is recorded rather than resolved. As with any mark, it is considered here alongside the vessel's form, ground and painting rather than as an isolated proof of date.

Optical Microscopy

The observations below concern the red enamel and turquoise glaze interface, a gilt-over-red enamel detail, the mark area, and the pink enamel of the floral panel, examined by optical microscopy. This section is limited to optical features; the elemental composition of the pigments and glazes is not addressed here.

Red Enamel / Turquoise Glaze Interface

Instrument Unconfirmed

A dark, glossy red-brown enamel meets a pale turquoise glaze along a soft, gradual boundary, with fine gilt striations visible running through the paler zone. The imaging device and exact magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Gilt Over Red Enamel

Instrument Unconfirmed

A band of gilt with a directional, leaf-like metallic texture runs beside a narrower red enamel line, both set against a pale ground; the gilt shows uneven reflectivity consistent with a hand-applied metallic layer. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Mark Area — Underglaze Cobalt on Turquoise Ground

Instrument Unconfirmed

A band of cobalt pigment of uneven density, with small bubble-like inclusions and bright reflective points, crosses a paler turquoise ground; the transition between the cobalt stroke and the surrounding glaze is soft rather than sharply defined. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Pink Enamel — Floral Panel

Instrument Unconfirmed

The pink enamel shows a granular, unevenly pigmented surface with a glossy sheen and uneven distribution of colour density across the field. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

This section is limited to optical observation of the surfaces shown above. The instrument and magnification used for these images are not documented, and this is noted accordingly.
