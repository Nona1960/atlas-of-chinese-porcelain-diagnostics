Ceremonial Vase with Pink Sgraffito Ground, Cranes and Famille-Rose Pheasant Medallion

乾隆年制 — Qiánlóng Nián Zhì · Famille-Rose (Fencai) Porcelain with Sgraffito Pink Ground, Qianlong Mark and Period (1736–1795)

Pink Sgraffito Ground Qing Dynasty · Qianlong 1736–1795 Jingdezhen Kilns (attributed) Cranes and Pheasant Medallion Underglaze-Blue Seal Mark on Turquoise Base Optical Microscopy Observations

Object Description

A vase with a lotus-petal rim, a neck and body covered in a pink enamel ground worked in the sgraffito technique — a fine scrolling pattern incised beneath the enamel, giving the surface a low-relief, textile-like quality where light catches the engraved lines. Set against this ground are numerous cranes in varied flying poses among clouds and floral scrolls, and, on the front of the vessel, a shaped white reserve panel containing a long-tailed pheasant among flowering peonies and bamboo.

Detail of the neck, handles and incised pink ground; and the base, showing a pale glaze and a seal-type mark within a square border.

The cranes are painted in varied poses without an obviously repeating silhouette, with fine individual feather strokes, which is consistent with hand painting across a large decorative field rather than a single repeated stencil. Within the reserve panel, the pheasant's plumage is built up in graduated dark-to-light tonal shading and the peony petals show soft internal colour gradation rather than flat single-tone fill, both typical of layered famille-rose enamel work.

The vase carries a pair of applied gilt handles at the shoulder. As photographed, these take a simple stacked open-ring form; the accompanying documentation describes handles of this type as modelled in dragon form. This is recorded as a discrepancy between the written description and the photographs rather than resolved in favour of either — a dragon-form handle and a plain ring handle are different features, and only the ring form is visible here.

The base is covered in a turquoise glaze and carries a mark in underglaze cobalt within a square border, described in the accompanying documentation as the four-character mark "Qianlong Nian Zhi" (乾隆年制). As with any reign mark, it is considered here alongside the vessel's form, ground and painting rather than as an isolated proof of date.

Optical Microscopy

The observations below concern the pink enamel, a gilt surface, and the purple-enamel gradation on the rim, examined by optical microscopy. This section is limited to optical features; the elemental composition of the pigments is not addressed here.

Pink Enamel — Transition Toward the Base

Instrument Unconfirmed

At this magnification, the pink enamel shows a glossy, finely crazed surface with fine hair-like inclusions, meeting a darker, matte, granular zone along an irregular boundary. The imaging device and exact magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Gilt Surface — Residual Gold

Instrument Unconfirmed

The gilt area shows scattered reflective metallic particles unevenly distributed across a worn, matte ground, consistent with a gilt layer that has thinned unevenly with handling; a red enamel border is visible along one edge of the field. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Purple Gradient Enamel — Lotus Rim

Instrument Unconfirmed

The purple enamel shows a gradual, feathered transition from a dense, dark tone into a paler ground, with small bubble-like inclusions concentrated in the deeper-coloured zone. As above, the imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

This section is limited to optical observation of the surfaces shown above. The instrument and magnification used for these images are not documented, and this is noted accordingly.
