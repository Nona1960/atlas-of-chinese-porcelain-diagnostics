Gilded Famille Rose Lidded Vase with Continuous Gold Ground

大清乾隆年製 — Dà Qīng Qiánlóng Nián Zhì · Early Qianlong Period, c. 1736–1745

Continuous Gold Ground Qing Dynasty · Early Qianlong c. 1736–1745 Jingdezhen Imperial Kilns (claimed) Famille Rose (Fencai) over Gilt Ground Turquoise Base & Seal Mark Dronova GOM Method

Object Description

The examined object is a closed, ovoid-bodied lidded vase with a short cylindrical neck and a domed lid, surmounted by a gilded teardrop-shaped finial set on a small pink-banded collar. The proportions are slender and restrained rather than boldly monumental — a quality the accompanying notes associate with the transition from the refined aesthetic of the late Yongzheng reign into the earliest phase of Qianlong-period production.

The entire visible surface of the vessel — body, neck, and lid alike — is covered by a continuous gold ground, without any white reserve panels; the famille rose floral decoration is painted directly over this gold field in soft, semi-transparent pastel enamels, so that the gold shows through the colour in places, producing an effect the notes compare to costly gold-brocade silk. The decorative programme is organised in registers: a wide floral-arabesque band at the shoulder with large peony and lotus blossoms, a band of heart-shaped lappets in pink and pale turquoise below the shoulder, further chrysanthemum and scrolling floral motifs down the body, and a narrow petal-lappet band above the foot; the lid repeats related floral motifs.

The pink tones throughout the floral painting show a soft, graduated, "watercolour" quality without hard colour boundaries, and are outlined in a very fine black contour line — consistent, at the level of ordinary photography, with the semi-transparent famille rose enamelling described in the notes, though the notes' specific claim that the pink is produced with a colloidal-gold (Purple of Cassius) pigment is a statement about chemical composition that cannot be confirmed from a photograph and is reported here as the notes' claim rather than as this entry's independent finding.

Technological Interpretation

The continuous gold ground, visible showing through the semi-transparent floral enamels in Figs. 1–2, is consistent with a hand-applied gilt background over which the famille rose painting was subsequently worked — a technically demanding, labour-intensive decorative method associated with the most costly grade of eighteenth-century imperial enamelled porcelain. In the standard photographs supplied, the gold shows a warm, slightly matte-to-satin lustre rather than a hard, uniform mirror shine, which is broadly consistent with a traditional low-fired gold rather than a modern sprayed or electroplated metallic finish, though this reading is visual rather than instrumentally confirmed.

The turquoise base glaze (Fig. 3) shows a smooth, evenly saturated surface with a slight, natural-looking pooling of colour toward the centre and a warm firing tone at the footring edge — features consistent with a genuine high-fired counter-glaze of the type documented elsewhere in this Atlas on Qianlong-period famille rose wares. The seal mark's stroke edges show a soft, slightly diffused quality into the surrounding glaze rather than a hard, mechanically sharp line, consistent with underglaze cobalt application, though — as with other entries in this Atlas relying on standard rather than magnified photography — this is a qualitative visual reading.

Gemmological Optical Microscopy

The accompanying notes for this object list an extensive, structured microscopy protocol — variation in gold-leaf thickness and overlap zones, the layered transparency of the famille rose enamels, the specific optical signature of Purple-of-Cassius pink, wear patterns in the gilding, the microstructure of the turquoise base glaze (micropores, iron inclusions), and cobalt diffusion in the reign mark — together with a recommended instrumental programme (UV fluorescence, XRF/µXRF for gold, lead, arsenic, and tin, and multilayer enamel microscopy). No magnified or microscope photographs, and no UV or XRF data, were supplied with this submission; only the three standard photographs reproduced above (Figs. 1–3) are available. Every microscope- or instrument-level claim in the notes is therefore recorded here as unconfirmed and is not repeated as an independent finding of this Atlas entry.

No microscopy or instrumental data available for this entry, despite a detailed proposed diagnostic protocol in the accompanying notes. To document this object properly under the Dronova GOM Method, the notes' own recommended programme should be carried out: magnified imaging of the gold ground (leaf-overlap and thickness variation), the pink famille rose enamel (layering and pigment structure), the turquoise base glaze (pore and inclusion structure), and the reign mark (cobalt diffusion), together with UV fluorescence and XRF/µXRF elemental analysis of the gold and enamels.

Diagnostic Checklist

  • Slender, restrained ovoid lidded form with a domed lid and gilded teardrop finial
  • Continuous gold ground covering the entire visible surface, with no white reserve panels
  • Famille rose floral painting in soft, graduated pastel enamels directly over the gold, with fine black contour lines
  • Registers of peony, lotus, chrysanthemum, and lappet motifs organising the decorative programme
  • Smooth, saturated turquoise base glaze with a natural firing tone at the footring
  • Underglaze-blue seal mark in a square cartouche with softly diffused stroke edges
  • Dating confirmed as early Qianlong (c. 1736–1745) on the basis of the reign mark, read as 大清乾隆年製 (Da Qing Qianlong Nian Zhi)
  • No microscopy or instrumental (UV/XRF) data supplied — the notes' extensive claims about gold-leaf structure, enamel layering, Cassius-pink chemistry, and mark cobalt diffusion are not confirmed by this entry
  • No elemental data yet obtained — the conclusion should be treated as preliminary at the chemical level
On the evidence of the three photographs supplied, the slender lidded form, the continuous hand-applied gold ground, the soft, semi-transparent famille rose floral painting, and the turquoise base with its underglaze-blue seal mark are consistent with a high-quality gilt-ground famille rose vase of the early Qianlong period (c. 1736–1745), a transitional moment that still carries the restrained elegance of the late Yongzheng aesthetic while introducing the richer decorative vocabulary that would define the mature Qianlong court style. The notes propose a substantial microscopy and instrumental programme (UV fluorescence, XRF/µXRF for gold and associated elements, multilayer enamel microscopy) that was not actually carried out or supplied as evidence, so its detailed technical claims are not repeated here as confirmed findings. A fully conclusive attribution would still benefit from the magnified and instrumental analysis outlined in the notes.
