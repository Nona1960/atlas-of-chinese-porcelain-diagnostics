Vase with Dark-Blue Ground, Celadon Relief Bands and a Long-Tailed Bird Medallion

乾隆年制 — Qiánlóng Nián Zhì (per accompanying documentation) · Famille-Rose (Fencai) Porcelain with Gilt Archaistic Handles, Qianlong Mark and Period (1736–1795)

Dark-Blue Ground with Gilt Scrollwork Qing Dynasty · Qianlong 1736–1795 Jingdezhen Kilns (attributed) Celadon Relief Lappet Bands Underglaze Mark, Diamond Cartouche Optical Microscopy Observations
Note on colour terminology: the accompanying documentation titles this object a "turquoise-ground" vase but its own body text describes the main ground as a "dark sapphire blue" enamel. As photographed, the main body ground is a dark navy-blue enamel with gilt scrollwork, while the neck and foot carry a pale, celadon-toned relief glaze closer to a turquoise-green tone; the base itself is also pale and celadon/turquoise-toned. The description below follows what is visible in the photographs rather than the title.

Object Description

A pear-shaped (yuhuchunping-related) vase with a widening neck and body. The neck and the foot are covered in a pale celadon glaze over a moulded relief of upright and inverted lappets, recalling the petal bands of archaic bronze vessels; between them runs a raised meander (leiwen) border picked out in gilt. The main body is covered in a dark navy-blue enamel ground overlaid with fine gilt scrollwork of lotus and peony vines, interrupted by a large shaped medallion on a white ground.

Detail of the handle area, where a small red pendant in the form of the "wan" (卐) character hangs beside the gilt handle; and a detail of the gilt scrollwork on the dark-blue ground, with a small applied gilt fish figure at the lower edge.

A pair of gilt handles in an archaistic hook-and-scroll form is applied at the shoulder; the small red wan-form pendant and the gilt fish figure noted above correspond to the fish-and-wan ornament described in the accompanying documentation as part of the handle decoration. Within the medallion, a long-tailed, crested bird is shown among peonies, blossoming branches and a smaller bird below; the accompanying documentation identifies this bird as a phoenix (鳳凰, fenghuang). As photographed, the bird's crest, banded tail and overall proportions read more as a naturalistic long-tailed pheasant than as the more composite, stylised form conventionally used for phoenix imagery in Qing court porcelain — this is noted as an open point of interpretation rather than resolved either way.

The base is covered in a pale, celadon/turquoise-toned glaze and carries a mark within a diamond-shaped (lozenge) cartouche, rendered in a soft blue-grey underglaze pigment, described in the accompanying documentation as a four-character mark reading "Qianlong Nian Zhi" (乾隆年制). As with any mark, it is considered here alongside the vessel's form, ground and painting rather than as an isolated proof of date.

Optical Microscopy

The observations below concern the pale relief glaze, the boundary between a red enamel element and the dark-blue ground, and a gilt surface, examined by optical microscopy. This section is limited to optical features; the elemental composition of the pigments and glazes is not addressed here.

Pale Relief Glaze

Instrument Unconfirmed

This image shows a pale, streaked surface with vertical striations of varying brightness; the specific decorative element photographed is not confirmed, and it is presented here as a general glaze-surface observation. The imaging device and exact magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Red Enamel / Dark-Blue Ground Boundary

Instrument Unconfirmed

A red enamel field, showing a fine granular surface with fibrous inclusions, meets a dark navy-blue enamel along a curved boundary with a fine dark line at the join. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Gilt Scrollwork Surface

Instrument Unconfirmed

The gilt surface shows a granular, unevenly worn texture with directional striations and scattered dark speckling, consistent with a metallic gilt layer viewed at an angle under strong reflected light. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

This section is limited to optical observation of the surfaces shown above. The instrument and magnification used for these images are not documented, and this is noted accordingly.
