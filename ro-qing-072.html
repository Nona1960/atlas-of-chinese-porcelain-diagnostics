Underglaze-Blue Vase with Applied Handles and Meander, Lotus and Vine Borders

大清乾隆年制 — Dà Qīng Qiánlóng Nián Zhì · Underglaze Cobalt-Blue (Qinghua) Porcelain Vase, Qianlong Mark and Period (1736–1795)

Underglaze Cobalt Blue (Qinghua) Qing Dynasty · Qianlong 1736–1795 Jingdezhen Kilns (attributed) Archaistic Form with Applied Handles Underglaze-Blue Seal Mark Optical Microscopy Observations

Object Description

A baluster-form vase with a short flared neck, compact globular body and a low straight foot, decorated throughout in underglaze cobalt blue beneath a clear glaze. The neck carries a key-fret (leiwen) border below the rim, a band of upright lappets, and a lotus-scroll register interrupted by a pair of small applied handles at the shoulder; the accompanying materials describe these handles as modelled in an elephant-head form, a decorative type associated with archaistic vase designs of the eighteenth century. The body is given over to a dense scrolling lotus-and-vine composition above a band of upright lappets near the foot.

Base with the underglaze mark, and a detail of the footrim showing the lappet border and glaze pooling near the foot.

The cobalt is applied with visible variation in tone — darker where the brush was reloaded, lighter in thinner passages — and the outlines of the lotus scroll and lappet bands show the soft diffusion into the surrounding glaze typical of underglaze blue fired at high temperature. The scrollwork is drawn freehand, without the mechanically repeating spacing seen in transfer-printed or stencilled imitations of this pattern.

The base, beneath a clear glaze, carries a seal-script mark within a square surround, reading (per the accompanying documentation) "Da Qing Qianlong Nian Zhi" (大清乾隆年制). The mark is rendered in the same underglaze cobalt as the body decoration, with locally denser concentrations of pigment. As with any seal-script reign mark of this type, it is considered here alongside the vessel's form, painting and glaze rather than as an isolated proof of date, since marks of this reading were used over a long period.

The footrim is unglazed on its resting surface and shows an even, warm-toned biscuit with surface wear consistent with long handling; no attempt has been made here to assess the age of this wear beyond noting its even distribution.

Optical Microscopy

The observation below concerns the underglaze cobalt-blue pigment and its boundary with the surrounding glaze, examined by optical microscopy. This section is limited to optical features; the elemental composition of the pigment is not addressed here.

Underglaze Cobalt Pigment Boundary

Instrument Unconfirmed

At the edge of a painted stroke, the cobalt pigment shows a gradual, feathered transition into the surrounding clear glaze rather than a hard printed edge, with fine granular concentration of pigment visible within the darker zone. The image was obtained at a magnification whose device and exact power are not documented, which limits the precision of interpretation; the observation is presented as preliminary and supplements, rather than replaces, direct examination.
