Miniature Clair-de-Lune Vase with Famille Rose Blossoming Branch and Poetic Inscription

大清乾隆年制 — Dà Qīng Qiánlóng Nián Zhì · Clair-de-lune Glazed Chamber Vase with Fencai Enamels and Calligraphic Inscription, Qianlong Mark and Period (1736–1795)

Clair-de-Lune (Yueh Pai) Glaze Qing Dynasty · Qianlong 1736–1795 Jingdezhen Kilns (attributed) Famille Rose Branch-and-Bird Motif Underglaze-Blue Seal Mark Optical Microscopy Observations

Object Description

A small baluster-form chamber vase with a flared neck and everted rim, covered in an even, pale lavender-toned monochrome glaze (clair de lune / yueh pai), a decorative type associated with the Jingdezhen kilns of the eighteenth century. One side carries an overglaze famille-rose (fencai) painting of a flowering branch with a perched bird; the opposite side bears a four-line black-enamel calligraphic inscription accompanied by two small red seals.

Inscribed side, and the base bearing the underglaze mark.

The painting is built up in layered, semi-transparent enamels with soft tonal gradation: the blossom petals are rendered in a gentle pink-white modelling, the branch in ochre-brown tones with dark contour lines, and the bird's plumage in fine layered strokes of dark-brown and black enamel. This manner of painting is typical of eighteenth-century court fencai work, which favoured smooth colour transitions without hard edges.

Macro photography of the painting and inscription (ordinary camera, not microscope) — details are visible, but these are not microscope images.

The inscription is executed in black enamel with visible uneven thickening of the brush strokes at their beginnings and ends and slight enamel bleed on the glaze surface; two small square red-enamel seals accompany the text. This combination of painting, calligraphy and seal reflects a decorative convention long associated with court-style porcelain, in which pictorial and literary elements are combined.

The base, beneath a clear glaze, carries a six-character mark reading "Da Qing Qianlong Nian Zhi" (大清乾隆年制) in seal script (zhuanshu), rendered in underglaze cobalt within a square arrangement, dense in tone with localised pigment concentration. The mark is consistent in form and placement with the type used on Qianlong-period court porcelain; as with any underglaze seal-script reign mark, it is read here alongside the vessel's form, glaze and painting rather than as an isolated proof-point.

Optical Microscopy

The observations below concern the glaze of the vessel, examined by optical gemmological microscopy — bubble morphology, the character of layer boundaries, and enamel structure. This section is limited to optical features; the elemental composition of the materials is not addressed here.

Glaze Bubble Structure

Optical Microscopy

Numerous bubbles of varying diameter are visible within the glaze body, unevenly distributed, with occasional larger inclusions among a finer bubble mass; some bubbles are elongated or flattened. Such an uneven bubble structure is consistent with a lead-silicate glaze fired under traditional kiln conditions, characteristic of the clair-de-lune glaze family associated with eighteenth-century Jingdezhen production.

Enamel / Glaze Interface Near the Seal

Instrument Unconfirmed

At the edge of the red seal enamel a grainy layer of pigment of uneven density is visible, transitioning gradually rather than sharply into the surrounding glaze; occasional larger pigment particles are distributed across the enamel surface. The image was obtained at a magnification whose device and exact power are not documented, which limits the precision of interpretation; the observation is presented as preliminary.

Painted Enamel Surface (Branch)

Instrument Unconfirmed

The surface of the dark branch enamel shows a fine-grained, slightly uneven texture. The image was taken at considerable magnification without confirmed capture parameters (equipment type, power), so the observation is indicative only and is presented as a supplement to the macro photographs rather than as independent diagnostic evidence.

This section is limited to optical observations of the glaze and enamels (bubble morphology, boundary character, surface grain). Images were obtained at differing magnifications and by different means; for some, the exact instrument and power are not documented, and this is noted individually.
