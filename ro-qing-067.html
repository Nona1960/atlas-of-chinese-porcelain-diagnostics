Bianhu Flask Vase with Famille Rose Court Figures

大清乾隆年製 — Dà Qīng Qiánlóng Nián Zhì (as claimed) · Imperial Famille Rose (Fencai) Porcelain, Qianlong Period (1736–1795)

Famille Rose (Fencai) Court Figures Qing Dynasty · Qianlong 1736–1795 Jingdezhen Imperial Kilns Bianhu (Flask) Form, Gilded Handles Pale Celadon-Green Base & Seal Mark Dronova GOM Method

Object Description

The examined object is a flattened, disc-bodied flask vase (bianhu) with a cylindrical neck and a pair of gilded, scroll-form handles rising from the shoulder to the neck. Both broad faces of the flask carry a shaped white reserve panel containing a hand-painted court scene in the famille rose (fencai) palette: Side A (Fig. 1) shows a standing scholar in a blue robe addressing a seated lady in pink beneath a stand of pine trees, with a low table and stool between them; Side B (Fig. 2) shows two seated women taking tea beside a garden rock, with a third figure reclining at the edge of the stone table. Facial features and drapery in both scenes are built up through multiple thin, semi-transparent enamel layers rather than flat colour blocks, with visible tonal gradation in the robes and controlled, fine brush outlines around the figures.

Surrounding both reserve panels, the neck and body are covered in a purple-pink ground patterned with a fine stippled diaper and overlaid with a hand-painted scrolling grapevine bearing gourds, melons, and small flowers in green, orange, and pink enamels, with gilded dots scattered across the vine — an auspicious combination (grapevine for abundance and numerous descendants, gourds for longevity) consistent with mid-Qing decorative conventions. The neck terminates in an orange-toned rim above a narrow key-fret-and-floral border, and the foot is finished with a green-and-orange lappet band.

The gilded handles show a warm, slightly worn metallic lustre with visible brush-edge irregularity where the gold meets the enamel ground, consistent with hand-applied gilding rather than a sprayed or plated finish, though — as elsewhere in this Atlas — this reading is based on visual appearance rather than instrumental testing.

Technological Interpretation

The court-figure painting on both sides shows the layered, semi-transparent enamel build-up and controlled tonal gradation in drapery associated with mature Qianlong-period famille rose figure painting, including a degree of spatial recession in the garden settings (trees and rockwork placed behind the seated figures) that is broadly consistent with the partial adoption of European-influenced pictorial space in Qianlong court workshops, though this object's use of that convention is modest compared to the most elaborate imperial figure paintings and should not be overstated.

The scrolling grapevine ground shows individually varied leaf shapes and multiple green tones with visible brush overlap at leaf edges, consistent with hand painting rather than a repeating transfer or stencilled pattern; the gilded dots along the vine appear, at the resolution of the supplied photographs, to sit on top of the surrounding enamel, consistent with gilding applied after the enamel firing.

A caution regarding the base: the accompanying notes for this object describe a "turquoise base" typical of Qianlong imperial wares, but the base as actually photographed (Fig. 3) is glazed in a pale, minty celadon-green tone rather than the more saturated turquoise seen on other objects in this Atlas (compare, for example, ro-qing-063 or ro-qing-065). This entry describes the colour actually visible in the photograph rather than repeating the "turquoise" description from the notes.

Fig. 3 — Base. An oval footring of buff-toned biscuit surrounds a pale, minty celadon-green glazed base — notably paler and cooler than the saturated turquoise counter-glaze seen on other objects in this Atlas. A small blue underglaze seal mark sits within a square cartouche off-centre within the oval; at the resolution of this photograph the mark is legible as a seal-script inscription in a square frame, but the individual characters are too small to read with confidence, so the six-character 大清乾隆年製 reading given in the accompanying notes is reported here as the client's claim rather than as independently confirmed from this image.

Gemmological Optical Microscopy

The accompanying notes for this object contain extensive claims about features visible "under magnification" — glaze micro-crazing and gas bubbles, multilayer enamel menisci and pigment particle irregularity, layered facial-modelling structure in the figure painting, individually applied leaves and gold in the grapevine ground, and cobalt diffusion in the reign mark. No magnified or microscope photographs were supplied with this submission; only the three standard photographs reproduced above (Figs. 1–3) are available. Every microscope-level claim in the notes is therefore recorded here as unconfirmed, and this Atlas entry does not repeat them as independently verified findings.

No microscopy imagery available for this entry, despite extensive microscope-level claims in the accompanying notes. To document this object properly under the Dronova GOM Method, magnified images would be needed of: (1) the glaze surface, to check for genuine age-related micro-crazing and bubble structure; (2) the pink and other pastel enamels in the figure painting, to assess layering and pigment granularity; (3) the gilded handles and vine dots, to assess hand-application versus a mechanical finish; and (4) the reign mark, to confirm both its full six-character reading and cobalt diffusion into the surrounding glaze.

Diagnostic Checklist

  • Flattened disc-bodied bianhu form with cylindrical neck and paired gilded scroll handles
  • Two hand-painted famille rose court scenes in shaped white reserve panels, one per face
  • Layered, semi-transparent enamel modelling of faces and drapery with fine, confident outlines
  • Hand-painted scrolling grapevine-and-gourd ground with individually varied leaves and gilded dots
  • Gilded handles with warm lustre and visually plausible hand-applied, worn gilding
  • Pale celadon-green (not saturated turquoise) glazed oval base, corrected against the accompanying notes
  • Blue underglaze seal mark present in a square cartouche; full six-character reading not confirmed at this image resolution
  • No microscopy imagery supplied — extensive microscope-level claims in the accompanying notes (crazing, enamel menisci, pigment granularity, gilding application, cobalt diffusion) are not confirmed by this entry
  • No elemental (XRF/SEM-EDS) data yet obtained — the conclusion should be treated as preliminary at the chemical level
On the evidence of the three photographs supplied, the bianhu form, the two hand-painted famille rose court scenes, the grapevine-and-gourd ground, and the gilded handles are consistent with well-executed mid-Qing famille rose porcelain of the general type associated with the Qianlong court. Two points from the accompanying notes are corrected rather than repeated in this entry: the base glaze is pale celadon-green in the supplied photograph, not the saturated turquoise described in the notes, and the individual characters of the reign mark are too small to confirm the claimed six-character 大清乾隆年製 reading at this image resolution. In addition, the notes' extensive microscope-level claims — glaze micro-crazing, enamel meniscus and pigment structure, gilding application, and cobalt diffusion in the mark — are not supported by any magnified image actually supplied, and are recorded here as unconfirmed rather than presented as findings. A conclusive attribution would require sharper, higher-resolution photography of the mark, genuine magnified imagery of the glaze and enamels, and elemental analysis (XRF/SEM-EDS) of the enamels, gilding, and body.
