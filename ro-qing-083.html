Clair-de-Lune Glazed Bottle Vase (Yuhuchunping)

Pear-shaped bottle vase (yuhuchunping): globular body, tall cylindrical neck with slightly everted lip, low ring foot; monochrome blue glaze of the type known as clair-de-lune / yuebai (月白釉); unglazed footrim; seal-form mark in underglaze blue on the glazed base.

Yuhuchunping Form Monochrome Blue Glaze Clair-de-Lune / Yuebai Underglaze-Blue Seal Mark Qing Dynasty

Object Description

The vase has a globular body rising into a tall, cylindrical neck with a slightly everted lip, standing on a low, gently spreading foot — the classic yuhuchunping (玉壺春瓶) profile. The surface is covered overall in a single-colour transparent glaze with a soft, silky sheen and a visibly even, unbroken tone across the body and neck.

Note
The accompanying documentation describes the glaze as "moon-white" (yuebai, 月白釉) / clair-de-lune, a term generally associated with a very pale, whitish-lavender blue. In the supplied photograph, the glaze reads as a fairly saturated, solid medium blue rather than the paler, more diffuse tone usually associated with that name. The glaze is described here by its visible colour and finish, with the "clair-de-lune / yuebai" label noted as the documentation's own designation rather than independently confirmed from the photograph.

The unglazed footrim is neatly finished, with a warm, light tan firing tone visible at the rim. The base is glazed and bears a small, square seal-form mark painted in underglaze blue, centred and set within the glazed field.

Note
The accompanying documentation reads this as a six-character Qianlong reign mark, 大清乾隆年製, in seal script. The mark's division into what appears to be a compact grid of character-units within the square is broadly consistent with a six-character seal-script arrangement, though individual strokes are not all fully resolved at the resolution supplied. The mark is recorded here per the accompanying documentation, and — as with any mark — it is considered together with the vessel's form and glaze rather than as an isolated proof of date or production site.

No photograph of the vessel's neck interior was supplied; the documentation's statement that the interior of the neck is glazed is noted here but was not independently checked against an image.

Optical Microscopy

The observations below concern the body glaze and a fragment of the cobalt mark, examined by optical/macro photography. This section is limited to optical features; the elemental composition of the glaze and pigment is not addressed here.

Body Glaze

Instrument Unconfirmed

A pale blue, low-contrast surface shows scattered small, soft-edged pale spots of varying size across the field, without sharply defined individual bubble outlines at this resolution. The image is fairly soft/out of focus, which limits how much of the fine surface texture can be resolved. The imaging device and exact magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Cobalt Mark, Fragment

Instrument Unconfirmed

A denser, darker-blue zone with a diffuse, irregular edge sits within a paler blue-white field; small rounded bright spots consistent with surface bubbles are scattered across both zones, somewhat more concentrated within the darker area. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary and limited to optical appearance.
