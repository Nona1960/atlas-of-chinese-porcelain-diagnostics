Iron-Rich Black Spots and Rust-Brown Glaze Transitions on a Yuan-Dynasty Qingbai Double-Gourd Ewer: Portable X-Ray Fluorescence Analysis and Optical Documentation

Nona Dronova — Independent Researcher — Zenodo, published 30 October 2026 (v1) — Archaeometry and Chinese Ceramic Technology

← Back to the Atlas entry ro-yuan-019 XRF protocols (PDF)

Abstract

This study presents archaeometric data on a Chinese qingbai porcelain ewer of the Yuan dynasty (1279–1368) in double-gourd form (hulu, 葫芦), decorated with characteristic iron-rich black spots and rust-brown glaze transitions.

Three areas of the vessel were examined by portable X-ray fluorescence spectroscopy (pXRF): the pale qingbai glaze, a black decorative spot, and a rust-brown area. The measurements revealed substantial differences in elemental iron concentration: 0.913 wt% Fe in the pale glaze, 5.722 wt% Fe in the black spot, and 1.161 wt% Fe in the rust-brown area.

The black decorative spot contains approximately 6.27 times more iron than the pale glaze. This pronounced enrichment establishes the iron-rich chemical character of the dark decoration.

Four photographs document the distribution of black spots, ochre-coloured glaze areas, pale glazed surfaces, and the unglazed part of the base. Particular attention is given to the irregular boundaries between coloured areas and to the clusters of dark inclusions occurring near these transitions.

The results are discussed in the context of published archaeometric studies of iron-rich decoration on Chinese qingbai porcelain, including studies of the microstructure and colouring mechanisms of iron spots from Jingdezhen.

This study makes an original contribution to the study of iron-bearing glazes used in the decoration of Yuan-dynasty Chinese porcelain by providing original elemental data and optical documentation.

Keywords: Yuan dynasty; qingbai porcelain; double-gourd ewer; hulu; iron-rich black spots; rust-brown glaze; Jingdezhen; portable X-ray fluorescence; pXRF; Chinese ceramic technology; archaeometry.

1. Introduction

Qingbai porcelain represents one of the principal achievements of Chinese high-temperature ceramic technology of the Song and Yuan dynasties. Its characteristic pale, translucent glaze was created through the interaction of silica-rich ceramic materials, feldspathic fluxes, and carefully controlled firing conditions.

Although qingbai porcelain is usually associated with a pale bluish-white or creamy surface, some historical vessels display additional decorative effects, including dark iron-rich spots and brown or ochre glaze areas.

Such features are particularly important for the study of medieval Chinese firing technology, because their appearance reflects changes in the distribution, concentration, and chemical behaviour of iron-bearing materials during high-temperature firing.

The present study concerns a Yuan-dynasty qingbai porcelain ewer in double-gourd, or hulu (葫芦), form.

The vessel has a pale glaze with scattered black decorative spots, broad rust-brown and ochre areas, and local transitions between these contrasting surface effects.

The aim of this study is to characterise the differences in elemental composition between three visually distinct areas of the vessel and to examine their relationship to published studies of the colouring mechanisms of iron-rich Chinese ceramics.

2. Historical and Art-Historical Context

2.1. The double-gourd form

The double-gourd form has a long history in Chinese material culture.

Known as hulu, it is associated with longevity, abundance, protection, and auspicious symbolism.

During the Yuan dynasty (1279–1368) the double-gourd form appeared in a variety of ceramic vessels, including ewers with curved spouts and handles.

The investigated vessel combines two rounded sections forming a vertically arranged double-gourd silhouette.

Its form includes a curved spout, an arched handle, and a small attachment loop.

The piece therefore belongs to a recognisable historical category of Chinese pouring vessels.

2.2. Qingbai glaze and iron-bearing decoration

Qingbai porcelain is characterised by a high-temperature silicate glaze whose colour can range from pale bluish-white to ivory or slightly greenish-white.

Iron, even in relatively small amounts, can influence the optical properties of these glazes.

Local concentrations of iron-bearing material can create brown, reddish-brown, or almost black decorative effects.

The technological study of such decoration is particularly important, because a single ceramic surface can show substantial differences in colour and elemental composition.

The investigated Yuan ewer is an example of this phenomenon.

3. Description of the Investigated Object

The investigated object is a Yuan-dynasty qingbai porcelain ewer in double-gourd form.

Its principal characteristics are:

  • A double-gourd body composed of two rounded sections.
  • A curved pouring spout.
  • An arched handle with a small attachment loop.
  • A pale white qingbai glaze.
  • Irregular black and dark-brown decorative spots.
  • Extensive transitions between ochre and rust-brown glaze.
  • An unglazed circular foot with visible surface features resulting from firing.

The dark decorative spots vary in size and position.

Some occur as isolated, relatively large features, while others form small scattered inclusions or clusters.

The rust-brown colouring forms broader areas that pass irregularly into the pale glaze.

The resulting surface demonstrates a complex relationship between the ceramic glaze and the iron-bearing decorative material.

The elemental composition of the vessel was examined by portable X-ray fluorescence spectroscopy.

The measurements were carried out on 28 October 2026 using the instrument with serial number 940487.

5.2. Iron enrichment of the black decorative spot

The principal analytical result is the pronounced iron enrichment of the black decorative area.

The measured iron concentrations are:

  • Pale qingbai glaze: 0.913 wt% Fe.
  • Black decorative spot: 5.722 wt% Fe.
  • Rust-brown area: 1.161 wt% Fe.

The iron enrichment factor between the black spot and the pale glaze is:

5.722 / 0.913 = 6.27

The black decorative spot therefore contains approximately 6.27 times more elemental iron than the pale glaze.

The iron enrichment factor between the black spot and the rust-brown area is:

5.722 / 1.161 = 4.93

These results show that the black decorative spot is an iron-rich area of the ceramic surface.

5.3. Iron-oxide equivalent

For comparison with ceramic literature in which iron is reported as Fe₂O₃, the measured elemental iron values can be expressed as theoretical Fe₂O₃ equivalents using the conversion factor 1.4297.

Table 2. Elemental iron content and theoretical Fe₂O₃ equivalent.

Surface area Fe (wt%) Fe₂O₃ equivalent (wt%)
Pale qingbai glaze 0.913 1.305
Black decorative spot 5.722 8.181

The Fe₂O₃ equivalent is a conventional calculation based on elemental iron content. It is not a mineralogical identification of hematite or any other specific iron-bearing phase.

The results provide a useful quantitative basis for comparison with published studies of iron-bearing Chinese ceramic glazes.

5.4. Manganese and other elements

Manganese is present in all three examined areas.

Its concentration reaches 1208 ppm in the black spot, compared with 997 ppm in the pale glaze and 799 ppm in the rust-brown area.

The increase in manganese accompanies the substantial iron enrichment.

However, the magnitude of the difference in iron content is much greater, identifying iron as the principal measured element associated with the black colour.

The black area also shows lower measured concentrations of silicon and potassium than the pale glaze.

These differences indicate compositional variation between the analysed surface areas.

6. Optical and Photographic Observations

Four photographic images were examined to characterise the surface morphology and the distribution of decorative colours.

6.1. Photograph 1: lower glazed surface

The first image records an irregular combination of pale glaze, ochre-coloured areas, and numerous small dark inclusions.

The dark particles occur both singly and in localised clusters.

Their distribution is particularly noticeable near the boundaries between pale and rust-brown areas.

Small irregularities and variations in texture are also visible on the surface.

6.2. Photograph 2: boundaries between pale and brown areas

The second image shows a complex, wavy boundary between the pale glaze and the rust-brown colouring.

Many small black spots lie close to this boundary.

This arrangement suggests local concentrations of dark decorative material within the broader brown zone.

The pale areas have irregular, flowing outlines that contrast visually with the surrounding ochre surface.

6.3. Photograph 3: magnified surface detail

The third image shows a magnified area of the glazed surface.

Dark-brown areas, pale translucent patches, and small surface irregularities are visible.

The image illustrates the heterogeneous character of the glaze and the gradual transitions between darker and lighter zones.

6.4. Photograph 4: lower body and foot

The fourth photograph shows the lower part of the vessel and its base from below.

It shows the relationship between the pale qingbai glaze, broad ochre areas, and individual black spots.

The unglazed foot is clearly visible.

This image provides important morphological context for interpreting the magnified photographs of the surface.

7. Comparison with Published Archaeometric Studies

7.1. Iron spots on Song qingbai porcelain

A particularly relevant study was published by Hu and colleagues in 2021: Microstructure and coloring mechanism of iron spots on Song-dynasty bluish-white porcelain from Jingdezhen.

The authors examined iron-rich spots on qingbai porcelain using complementary microstructural and spectroscopic methods.

The study established relationships between iron concentration, iron-bearing crystalline phases, and visible colour.

Complex iron-oxide structures associated with the formation of the characteristic iron spots were identified.

The present Yuan ewer shows a similar relationship between iron enrichment and local dark colouring.

The measured iron content of the black decorative spot, 5.722 wt%, provides a quantitative basis for this comparison.

7.2. Iron and the colour of qingbai glaze

Studies of qingbai ceramics have shown that iron plays an important role in determining glaze colour.

The optical effects depend not only on total iron concentration, but also on the oxidation state of iron, its local chemical environment, and the glaze microstructure.

These factors contribute to colour variations: pale, greenish, yellowish, brown, and dark.

The investigated ewer displays several of these visual effects on the same ceramic surface.

The pXRF results establish the distribution of elements associated with this variation.

7.3. Iron-bearing spots in later Chinese porcelain

Further comparative information can be obtained from studies of iron spots on Jingdezhen blue-and-white porcelain.

Microstructural studies have identified several iron-bearing crystalline phases in dark spots, including magnetite and complex iron-bearing silicates.

Although these ceramic traditions differ in their decorative techniques, the research demonstrates the importance of iron-rich phases in forming dark ceramic surface patterns.

The present study provides a comparative elemental analysis of a Yuan-dynasty qingbai vessel.

8. Discussion

The combination of X-ray fluorescence measurements and photographic documentation provides a coherent picture of the iron-rich decorative system of the investigated ewer.

The pale qingbai glaze contains 0.913 wt% elemental iron.

The black decorative spot shows a markedly higher iron concentration of 5.722 wt%.

The rust-brown area contains 1.161 wt% iron.

These differences correspond to the visually distinct colour zones observed on the vessel.

The most pronounced iron enrichment is found in the black spot, where the concentration is approximately 6.27 times higher than in the pale glaze.

The rust-brown area occupies an intermediate position in iron concentration, although its colour differs substantially from the pale ground.

The photographic evidence further confirms that the dark spots take several morphological forms: isolated spots, small clusters, and scattered inclusions near broader ochre-coloured areas.

This combination of chemical and visual characteristics is important for understanding the relationship between iron-bearing decorative materials and glaze behaviour during high-temperature ceramic production.

The historical double-gourd form provides the art-historical context of the object, while the analytical measurements document the chemical characteristics of its decoration.

Together, these results contribute to the technological study of Yuan-dynasty qingbai porcelain.

9. Conclusions

This study presents the results of non-destructive elemental analysis and optical documentation of a Yuan-dynasty qingbai porcelain ewer in double-gourd form, decorated with iron-rich black spots and rust-brown glaze transitions.

The investigation established the following:

  • Three visually distinct surface areas were examined by portable X-ray fluorescence spectroscopy.
  • The pale qingbai glaze contains 0.913 wt% iron.
  • The black decorative spot contains 5.722 wt% iron.
  • The rust-brown area contains 1.161 wt% iron.
  • The black spot shows approximately 6.27-fold iron enrichment relative to the pale glaze.
  • The theoretical Fe₂O₃ equivalent of the black area is approximately 8.18 wt%.
  • The photographic documentation reveals isolated black spots, clusters of dark inclusions, broad ochre-coloured areas, and irregular transitions between glaze colours.

The results provide an experimental basis for comparison with published studies of iron-bearing spots and colouring mechanisms in Chinese qingbai ceramics.

This study makes an original analytical contribution to the study of the iron-bearing decorative technology of medieval Chinese porcelain.

The combination of quantitative elemental measurements, photographic evidence, and comparative archaeometric research provides a detailed characterisation of the vessel’s distinctive black and rust-brown glaze effects.
