Vase with Celadon Sgraffito Ground and Red Famille-Rose Cartouches

乾隆御製 — Qiánlóng Yù Zhì · Famille-Rose (Fencai) Porcelain with Celadon Sgraffito Ground, Qianlong Mark and Period (1736–1795)

Celadon Sgraffito Ground Qing Dynasty · Qianlong 1736–1795 Jingdezhen Kilns (attributed) Red Famille-Rose Cartouches Underglaze Mark 乾隆御製 Optical Microscopy Observations

Object Description

A baluster vase covered in a pale celadon glaze over a continuous incised (sgraffito) scrolling ground, visible as a low-relief pattern beneath the translucent glaze. A large shaped red cartouche on the body is painted in famille-rose enamels with a pair of birds among peonies, magnolia and bamboo; a smaller red diamond-shaped cartouche near the rim carries a single gilt-outlined character read in the accompanying documentation as "shou" (寿, longevity). A pair of gilt scroll-form handles is applied at the shoulder.

Detail of the cartouche border against the incised celadon ground; and the base, painted with sprigs of flowers around a square underglaze mark.

Within the red cartouche, the birds and magnolia blossoms are built up with graduated shading and fine gilt highlighting on the feathers, and the incised diaper beneath the surrounding celadon glaze is visible as a continuous, evenly scaled scroll rather than a repeating printed unit. The gilt handles are scrolled in outline; the accompanying documentation describes handles of this type as modelled in relief as dragons, but at the resolution of the photographs available here no clearly draconic features (head, claws) can be confirmed, so this is left as an open point rather than affirmed.

The base is covered in the pale celadon-toned glaze and painted with a few sprigs of flowers around a square mark rendered in underglaze blue, reading "Qianlong Yu Zhi" (乾隆御製, "made by imperial order of Qianlong") — a mark type distinct from the more common "Da Qing Qianlong Nian Zhi" reign mark seen elsewhere in this atlas. As with any mark, it is considered here alongside the vessel's form, ground and painting rather than as an isolated proof of date or of palace origin.

Optical Microscopy

The observations below concern the underglaze cobalt of the mark, the red enamel and body interface, a gilt border, and the celadon anhua ground, examined by optical microscopy. This section is limited to optical features; the elemental composition of the pigments and glazes is not addressed here.

Underglaze Cobalt (Mark)

Instrument Unconfirmed

The cobalt strokes show a soft, feathered edge diffusing into the surrounding clear glaze rather than a hard printed boundary, with a fairly even tone across the stroke width. The imaging device and exact magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Red Enamel and Body Interface

Instrument Unconfirmed

A granular, unglazed body area meets the red enamel along a fairly sharp boundary, with fine dark particulate matter scattered across the body surface. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Border Area — Red Enamel on Unglazed Ground

Instrument Unconfirmed

The red enamel forms angular fields against a granular tan ground, with a glossy sheen visible on the enamel surface and fine light-coloured mineral particles scattered in the surrounding matrix. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Celadon Glaze — Anhua Relief Ground

Optical Microscopy

The celadon glaze shows a smooth, glossy surface with a faint wave-like relief pattern visible beneath it, consistent with the incised (anhua) decoration described for this ground, and without the sharp, uniform edges that would indicate a moulded or printed substitute. Magnification and device are not separately confirmed for this image and the observation is presented as descriptive rather than conclusive.

This section is limited to optical observation of the surfaces shown above. The instrument and magnification used for these images are not documented, and this is noted accordingly.
