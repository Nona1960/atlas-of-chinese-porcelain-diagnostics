Monumental Celadon-Ground Vase with Famille Rose Landscape Medallions

乾隆年製 — Qiánlóng Nián Zhì · Imperial Famille Rose (Fencai) Porcelain on Celadon Ground, Qianlong Mark and Period (1736–1795)

Famille Rose (Fencai) Shan-Shui Medallions Qing Dynasty · Qianlong 1736–1795 Jingdezhen Imperial Kilns Pale Celadon Ground & Key-Fret Diaper Underglaze-Blue Seal Mark Dronova GOM Method

Object Description

The examined object is a monumental porcelain vase of tall ceremonial proportions, with a swelling lower body, a controlled waist transition, and a flared mouth with precisely finished rim — a balanced, strongly axially symmetrical silhouette characteristic of court display porcelain of the Qianlong reign (1736–1795), produced at the Imperial kilns of Jingdezhen. The surface is structured by a refined decorative system: a pale, translucent celadon ground overlaid with a key-fret (leiwen) diaper network and small rosette nodes at the structural intersections, providing both chromatic support and a structural framework for the pictorial medallions set within it.

The principal decorative zones are large oval landscape medallions executed in the shan-shui tradition, showing layered mountain formations with atmospheric perspective, ink-like sepia and black contouring, mineral green and blue washes on distant peaks, and mist effects created by diluted enamel transitions. Within these landscapes, structured narrative scenes unfold: a scholar or traveller in blue or purple robes crossing an arched stone bridge, pavilion structures with tiled roofs, a fishing boat on a calm river surface, birds in flight, and — in one medallion — what appears to be a robed figure with a deer, all rendered with controlled spatial scaling between figures and setting.

The neck and shoulder carry dense lotus scrolls in multicoloured enamel layers, with pink-to-magenta tonal gradients, turquoise and lemon-yellow highlights, and small cartouches integrated into the scrolling system, all outlined in fine black and iron-red contour drawing. The lower body is stabilised by a sequence of layered bands: a cresting wave register with articulated white foam, a lotus-petal (lappet) frieze beneath it, a secondary key-fret border, and dotted running-motif separation lines — a coherent, hierarchical border grammar typical of large-format imperial vases of this period.

Fig. 6 — Base. Fully glazed celadon-aquamarine base within a neatly cut footring showing controlled biscuit exposure and natural firing tone. A blue underglaze seal mark sits within a square cartouche at the centre; as photographed, the mark is partially obscured by a bright reflection, but the legible characters appear to read 乾隆御製 (Qianlong Yuzhi, four characters) rather than the six-character 大清乾隆年製 (Da Qing Qianlong Nian Zhi) mark described in the accompanying notes for this object — both are attested Qianlong mark types, and the description here follows what can actually be read in the photograph.

Technological Interpretation

The famille-rose (fencai) enamel system used across the neck scrolls, border bands, and landscape medallions shows a semi-transparent, pastel modulation with controlled variation in enamel thickness and a layered application of colour fields, with soft blending at tonal transitions and an absence of the mechanical uniformity that would suggest a printed or transfer-based decoration. The pink enamels in particular are typical of the opacified, lead-arsenate-type pink system conventionally associated with mature Qing famille-rose technology, though — as with the pigment reading elsewhere in this Atlas — the specific chemistry cannot be confirmed from a photograph alone.

The enamel surface shows a glossy finish with fine micro-variation, a slight relief in the more densely applied pink and yellow fields, and stable adhesion between the enamel and the underlying glaze, with no evidence of post-firing overpainting. The layered border system — key-fret diaper, ruyi and lotus-scroll bands, wave register, and lappet frieze — is executed with a consistent hierarchy of scale and colour across the full circumference of the vessel, a level of compositional discipline consistent with a large-format palace commission rather than a later or provincial imitation.

The pale celadon ground itself, visible both around the medallions and beneath the diaper pattern, provides a translucent chromatic base against which the denser famille-rose pigments are set — a combination of an underglaze monochrome celadon field with overglaze polychrome painting that reflects the technical range available to the imperial workshops at the height of Qianlong-period production.

Gemmological Optical Microscopy

Two magnified images were supplied for this object: a close view of a pink enamel field within one of the decorative borders, and a view of a glaze/body boundary near the footring showing a faint trace of underglaze blue. Both show useful resolvable detail; neither carries a scale marker, so the observations below are qualitative.

Zone A — Pink Enamel Surface within a Border Field

Close-up / Magnification unconfirmed

Several rounded fields of a saturated pink-red enamel, each showing a visibly granular, glass-like internal texture, are separated by dark, slightly raised contour lines against a pale cream ground. This granularity and the raised quality of the contour lines are consistent with fired overglaze enamel applied within a hand-drawn outline, in keeping with the multilayer, hand-painted decoration described macroscopically above.

Zone B — Glaze/Body Boundary near the Footring, with Underglaze-Blue Trace

Close-up / Magnification unconfirmed

A pale, near-white glazed field at left meets a warmer, reddish-orange banded zone at right — likely a firing-related tonal variation at or near the footring — with a faint, softly diffused blue mark visible at lower left. The diffusion pattern of the blue trace is soft rather than sharply printed, consistent with an underglaze cobalt application, though this single frame cannot on its own confirm whether it belongs to the reign mark or to a separate decorative element nearby.

Diagnostic Checklist

  • Tall, ceremonial, strongly axially symmetrical silhouette typical of monumental court display porcelain
  • Pale, translucent celadon ground structured by a key-fret (leiwen) diaper network with rosette nodes
  • Large oval shan-shui landscape medallions with layered mountains, atmospheric perspective, and mineral washes
  • Structured narrative scenes (travellers, bridges, pavilions, boats) with controlled spatial scaling
  • Dense, multicoloured lotus scrolls at neck and shoulder with fine iron-red and black contour drawing
  • Hierarchical lower-body border system: wave band, lappet frieze, secondary key-fret, dotted lines
  • Layered, semi-transparent famille-rose enamel system with soft tonal blending, no mechanical uniformity
  • Fully glazed celadon-aquamarine base with a neatly cut footring and controlled biscuit exposure
  • Blue underglaze seal mark in a square cartouche — legible as a four-character 乾隆御製 mark in the supplied photograph
  • Magnified images show a granular fired-enamel texture and a plausible soft underglaze-blue diffusion near the footring; imaging device and magnification unconfirmed
  • No elemental (XRF/SEM-EDS) data yet obtained — the conclusion should be treated as preliminary at the chemical level
The monumental, axially symmetrical form, the structured celadon-and-key-fret ground, the high-level shan-shui narrative painting in the famille-rose palette, the coherent hierarchical border system, and the morphology of the underglaze-blue seal mark together are consistent with a large-format imperial commission of the Qianlong period, produced at the Jingdezhen kilns. As noted at Fig. 6, the seal mark as photographed reads as four characters (乾隆御製, Qianlong Yuzhi) rather than the six-character Da Qing Qianlong Nian Zhi mark referenced in the accompanying notes; this discrepancy is recorded rather than resolved in favour of either reading. The two supplied magnified images support the macroscopic reading at a qualitative level — a granular, fired pink-enamel texture and a plausibly genuine underglaze-blue trace near the footring — but the imaging device and magnification could not be independently confirmed. A conclusive attribution would additionally require elemental analysis (XRF/SEM-EDS) of the enamels, celadon ground, and body, together with direct, unobstructed photography of the complete reign mark.
