QING-QL-YSG-066  ·  ro-qing-066

Covered Meiping-Type Jar with Yellow Sgraffito Ground and Famille Rose Painting

大清乾隆年製 — Dà Qīng Qiánlóng Nián Zhì · "Spring on Yellow Silk II" · Imperial Yangcai/Famille Rose Porcelain, Qianlong Period (1736–1795)

Yellow Enamel Ground with Hand-Cut Sgraffito Qing Dynasty · Qianlong 1736–1795 Jingdezhen Imperial Kilns Famille Rose (Fencai) Bird-and-Flower Painting Turquoise-Glazed Base & Seal Mark Dronova GOM Method

Object Description

The object documented here is a covered, meiping-type jar decorated on a continuous imperial-yellow overglaze enamel ground, into which a fine spiral scroll diaper has been hand-engraved (sgraffito) beneath the yellow enamel — a laborious technique that produces a subtle, silk-like shimmer across the ground as light catches the incised pattern. Only a domed lid with shoulder (Fig. 1), a base view (Fig. 2), and a close floral detail (Fig. 3) were supplied; no photograph of the complete standing vessel is available, so the overall body form and total height cannot be described from the images at hand.

The lid is surmounted by a pomegranate-form finial in iron-red enamel, and the visible shoulder is painted, over the yellow sgraffito ground, with a spray of white-and-magenta peach blossom, a large white peony with pink-flecked petals, and a long-tailed pheasant rendered in layered brown, blue, and iron-red plumage — a bird-and-flower composition consistent with the "flowers of the four seasons" and pheasant-and-peony conventions common on high-quality Qianlong-period famille rose porcelain.

In the close floral detail (Fig. 3), the individual petals of a large white peony bloom show graduated pink shading toward the petal edges, with fine black-brown contour lines separating each petal and a visible, slightly uneven brushstroke quality in the green leaves — consistent with hand-applied, multilayer famille rose enamelling rather than a flat, single-pass colour fill.

Technological Interpretation

The continuous yellow ground with an engraved spiral diaper beneath it is consistent with the "brocade-ground" (jinshang tianhua) sgraffito technique used on the finest Qianlong-period imperial enamelled wares, in which a fine scroll pattern is incised into the enamel surface before or during firing and becomes visible chiefly through the play of reflected light across its texture, rather than through added colour. In Fig. 2, the incised scrollwork is visible as a subtle raised-and-recessed texture running continuously beneath and around the painted flowers, without any obvious interruption or mismatch where the floral painting overlaps it — consistent with the engraving having been executed as part of the ground layer before the overglaze floral painting was added, rather than being scratched into a finished painted surface.

The base is covered in a turquoise counter-glaze of the type seen elsewhere in this Atlas on Qianlong-period famille rose wares, and the seal mark sits within a clearly demarcated square cartouche at the centre; specific claims about cobalt penetration depth or the absence of "digital printing" made in the accompanying notes are plausible in general terms for a genuinely hand-produced underglaze mark but cannot be verified from a standard photograph and are not repeated here as confirmed findings.

The pomegranate-form finial and the surrounding painted decoration on the lid show the same enamel palette and painting quality as the shoulder below, suggesting the lid and body were decorated as a matched unit rather than assembled from mismatched parts — though, again, this is a visual impression from the photograph rather than a physical-fit examination.

Gemmological Optical Microscopy

The accompanying notes for this object describe a range of features said to be visible "under the microscope" — variable engraving depth in the sgraffito ground, granular mineral pigment in the contour lines, colloidal-gold depth in the pink enamels, and the absence of digital-printing artefacts in the reign mark. No magnified or microscope photographs were in fact supplied with this submission; only the three standard close-up photographs reproduced above (Figs. 1–3) are available. The claims specific to microscopic examination are therefore recorded here as unconfirmed pending actual magnified imagery, and are not presented as findings of this Atlas entry.

No microscopy imagery available for this entry, despite microscope-level claims in the accompanying notes. To properly document this object under the Dronova GOM Method, magnified images would be needed of: (1) the engraved sgraffito ground itself, to confirm variable incision depth and hand-tool irregularity; (2) the pink famille-rose enamel, to assess granularity and colour depth; (3) the grey-black contour lines, to assess mineral pigment granularity; and (4) the reign mark, to assess cobalt diffusion into the surrounding glaze.

Diagnostic Checklist

  • Continuous imperial-yellow enamel ground with a fine, hand-engraved spiral scroll diaper visible as a light-reflective texture
  • Famille rose bird-and-flower painting (peach blossom, peony, long-tailed pheasant) with graduated shading and fine contour lines
  • Domed lid with pomegranate-form iron-red finial, decorated en suite with the shoulder below
  • Turquoise-glazed base with a blue underglaze seal mark in a square cartouche
  • No photograph of the complete standing vessel supplied — overall form and proportions not independently assessed
  • No microscopy imagery supplied — engraving-depth, pigment-granularity, and cobalt-diffusion claims in the accompanying notes are not confirmed by this entry
  • No elemental (XRF/SEM-EDS) data yet obtained — the conclusion should be treated as preliminary at the chemical level
On the evidence of the three photographs supplied, the yellow sgraffito ground, the famille-rose bird-and-flower painting, and the turquoise-glazed, seal-marked base are consistent with high-quality Qianlong-period imperial enamelled porcelain of the type produced at the Jingdezhen kilns. This entry is necessarily partial: no image of the complete standing vessel was provided, so its overall form, height, and proportions are not described here, and the extensive microscope-level claims in the accompanying notes (engraving-depth variation, mineral pigment granularity, colloidal-gold enamel depth, absence of digital-printing artefacts in the mark) could not be verified, since no magnified photographs were actually supplied. A conclusive attribution would require a full-vessel photograph, magnified imagery of the sgraffito ground and enamels, and elemental analysis (XRF/SEM-EDS) of the glazes and pigments.
