Imperial Doucai Vase with Bat Handles and Ruyi Emblems

大清乾隆年製 — Dà Qīng Qiánlóng Nián Zhì · Imperial Doucai Porcelain, Qianlong Period (1736–1795)

Doucai Technique (鬥彩) Qing Dynasty · Qianlong 1736–1795 Jingdezhen Imperial Kilns Bat Handles & Suspended Ruyi Emblems Underglaze-Blue Seal Mark Dronova GOM Method

Object Description

The examined vase belongs to the finest group of imperial doucai porcelains produced at the Imperial kilns of Jingdezhen during the Qianlong reign (1736–1795). The vessel combines a cylindrical body with a tall neck, vertically raised panels, sculptural bat-shaped handles, and suspended ruyi emblems. The refined, balanced proportions and the precisely finished, thin porcelain body correspond to ceremonial imperial wares produced for the Qing court.

Decoration is executed in the classical doucai technique, in which underglaze cobalt outlines are completed with overglaze polychrome enamels applied during a second, low-temperature firing. The cobalt contours beneath the glaze show natural variation in brushstroke width and a slight diffusion of the pigment into the glaze, with smooth integration into the glassy matrix — features consistent with hand-painted underglaze decoration executed prior to the high-temperature firing, rather than a printed or mechanically reproduced outline.

The overglaze enamels — brilliant green, yellow, iron-red, aubergine, and pink — are applied in multiple layers, with smooth colour transitions, visible hand-controlled brushwork, and localised accumulation at the edges of each compartment. Each colour occupies its own area of the design while maintaining a natural continuity along the underglaze contour lines, with precise registration between the blue outline and the later-applied colour fill and no visible displacement between the two decorative layers.

Fig. 4 — Base. Smooth biscuit surface within the unglazed footring; at the centre, a six-character underglaze-blue seal mark 大清乾隆年製 (Da Qing Qianlong Nian Zhi) with sharp calligraphic execution and controlled cobalt penetration beneath the glaze.

Technological Interpretation

The body is divided into raised vertical panels formed before glazing, with uniform panel spacing, sharply defined relief edges, and smooth glaze continuity across the relief, including a natural pooling of glaze adjacent to the raised borders. No moulding defects were identified, and the relief structure remains fully integrated beneath the transparent glaze — consistent with the panel decoration having been shaped in the body itself rather than added as a later surface treatment.

The bat and ruyi motifs applied to the neck were modelled before glazing and fired together with the vessel body, producing a continuous ceramic structure: the join between each sculptural element and the wall is seamless, glaze coverage continues across the join without interruption, and no traces of secondary adhesive or restoration were identified at the attachment points.

The transparent glaze itself shows a homogeneous vitreous matrix, high optical transparency, and even thickness, with a strong vitreous gloss and a smooth interaction with the underglaze cobalt — allowing both the blue contour lines and the underlying relief modelling to remain clearly visible beneath the glaze layer, without any secondary surface coating.

Gemmological Optical Microscopy

No magnified or microscope photographs of the glaze, enamel, or cobalt layers were supplied for this object. The observations in this Atlas entry are therefore based entirely on macroscopic (naked-eye and standard close-up photographic) examination, as documented in Figs. 1–4. This section is included for structural consistency with other entries in the Atlas and should be read as pending rather than complete.

No microscopy imagery available for this entry. A gemmological optical microscopy pass — in particular of the cobalt/glaze interface at the underglaze contours and of the enamel surface at colour-compartment boundaries — is recommended before this object is treated as fully documented under the Dronova GOM Method.

Diagnostic Checklist

  • Cylindrical body with balanced proportions and a precisely finished, thin porcelain profile
  • Vertically raised panels with uniform spacing and sharply defined, glaze-integrated relief edges
  • Underglaze cobalt contours with natural brushstroke variation and slight in-glaze diffusion
  • Overglaze polychrome enamels (green, yellow, iron-red, aubergine, pink) with precise registration to the cobalt outline
  • Individually modelled bat handles and suspended ruyi emblems, seamlessly fired with the body
  • Continuous glaze coverage across sculptural joins, with natural glaze thickening around applied details
  • Homogeneous, transparent, high-gloss glaze with even thickness and no secondary coating
  • Neatly finished unglazed footring, dense white porcelain body, smooth biscuit surface
  • Underglaze-blue six-character seal mark Da Qing Qianlong Nian Zhi with sharp calligraphy
  • No microscopy imagery supplied — glaze/enamel microstructure not independently verified
  • No elemental (XRF/Raman/SEM-EDS) data yet obtained — the conclusion should be treated as preliminary
The macroscopic evidence — refined imperial proportions, the classical doucai sequence of underglaze cobalt completed by overglaze enamels, the multilayer enamel structure, the naturally integrated relief panels, the sculptural bat handles with suspended ruyi emblems, and the morphology of the underglaze-blue reign mark — is consistent with high-quality imperial doucai porcelain of the Qianlong period, produced at the Imperial kilns of Jingdezhen. No microscopy imagery was supplied for this object, so the glaze and enamel microstructure could not be independently examined at magnification; this entry should be treated as macroscopically consistent but not yet confirmed at the microscopic or elemental level. A conclusive attribution would require gemmological optical microscopy of the cobalt/glaze interface and enamel surface, together with elemental analysis (XRF/SEM-EDS) of the glaze, enamels, and body.
