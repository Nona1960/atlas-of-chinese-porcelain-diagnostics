Vase with Sages in a Garden and Famille-Rose Decoration

大清乾隆年制 — Dà Qīng Qiánlóng Nián Zhì · Famille-Rose (Fencai) Porcelain Vase with Figural Garden Scene, Qianlong Mark and Period (1736–1795)

Famille Rose (Fencai) Figural Scene Qing Dynasty · Qianlong 1736–1795 Jingdezhen Kilns (attributed) Hexagonal Neck, Applied Handles Underglaze-Blue Seal Mark Optical Microscopy Observations

Object Description

A vase with a tall hexagonal neck opening onto a broad ovoid body, the neck faceted into six panels carrying a pink-ground diaper with a yellow-ground floral cartouche, and the body given over to a garden scene painted in famille-rose enamels: two seated figures at a table beneath a large tree, with rockwork and a landscape ground visible behind them. A pair of applied handles rises from the shoulder to the neck on either side.

Detail of a neck cartouche and one applied handle.

The figural scene is painted with individualised faces and drapery rendered through soft tonal shading rather than hard outline alone, and the brushwork of the two figures differs enough from one another to indicate freehand painting rather than a repeated stencilled pattern. The neck cartouches show a dense, evenly scaled scrolling ground pattern beneath the floral medallions, painted in fine line rather than printed dot-matrix work.

The applied handles are rendered in a metallic bronze-gold tone that is visually distinct from the surrounding enamelled porcelain surface; the accompanying documentation describes these as separately modelled dragon-form handles integrated into the body before glazing. Based on the photographs alone it cannot be confirmed whether the handles are glazed porcelain or an applied metal (e.g. gilt-bronze) mount — the two would be diagnosed differently, and this is recorded here as an open question rather than resolved either way.

Optical Microscopy

The observations below concern three enamelled and glazed surfaces on the vessel, examined by optical microscopy. This section is limited to optical features; the elemental composition of the pigments and glazes is not addressed here.

Purple and Black Enamel — Layered Brushwork

Instrument Unconfirmed

At this magnification, the black enamel shows a fine granular texture with scattered light-reflecting particles, meeting the adjacent purple/pink enamel along an irregular, feathered boundary rather than a sharp printed edge, with a thin pale line visible where the two layers overlap. The imaging device and exact magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Brown Enamel — Mineral Pigment Granularity

Instrument Unconfirmed

The brown enamel surface shows a coarse, uneven granular texture with directional striations, consistent with a mineral-pigmented enamel viewed under raking light. As above, the imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Turquoise Base Glaze — Crystalline Inclusions

Instrument Unconfirmed

The turquoise glaze shows scattered small dark inclusions and bright reflective points distributed unevenly through a translucent blue-green matrix. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

This section is limited to optical observation of the enamel and glaze surfaces shown above. The instrument and magnification used for these images are not documented, and this is noted accordingly.
