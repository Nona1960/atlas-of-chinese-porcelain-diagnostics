Ceremonial Vase with Peaches, Clouds, and Elephant-Head Handles

Large ovoid vase with a lotus-petal crown mouth painted in cobalt blue; an imperial-yellow ground with peach branches and stylised clouds in blue grisaille; a lower band of green-and-white stylised wave/mountain motifs; gilded elephant-head handles with turquoise-painted eyes; a celadon-glazed base bearing a square underglaze-blue seal mark.

Imperial Yellow Ground Blue Grisaille Clouds & Peaches Lotus-Petal Crown Gilt Elephant-Head Handles Qing Dynasty

Object Description

The vase has a large ovoid body, a neck banded in blue lappets on a yellow ground, and a flaring mouth formed as a crown of pointed petals painted in cobalt blue with a fine radiating line pattern. Two gilded, sculptural elephant-head handles with turquoise-painted eyes and curled trunks are applied at the neck-to-shoulder junction.

The main yellow-ground register shows branches bearing rounded fruit (consistent with peaches) rendered in blue monochrome (grisaille) line-work, interspersed with stylised blue cloud forms. A lower register shows a band of stylised green-and-white wave and rock/mountain forms. A narrow band of small floral roundels appears at the shoulder, above the elephant handles.

The base is glazed a pale celadon-green and bears a small, square seal-form mark in underglaze blue.

Major Discrepancy
The accompanying documentation titles this object "Tongzhi Period (1862–1874)" and states the base mark reads "正製御窑" ("Made in the Imperial Kilns"). The photograph of the base, however, clearly shows a different four-character mark: 雍正御製 (Yongzheng yuzhi, "Imperially made in the Yongzheng reign"), referring to the Yongzheng Emperor (r. 1723–1735) — a different reign roughly 140 years earlier than Tongzhi, and a different set of characters than the documentation quotes. This is a direct contradiction between the documentation's stated period/mark and what is visible in the photograph. This entry records the mark as it actually reads in the photograph (雍正御製) and does not adopt the documentation's "Tongzhi period" title or its quoted mark text. As with any mark, this reading is noted alongside the vessel's form and decoration rather than treated as a settled proof of date — the mark alone does not resolve which period the piece belongs to, especially given this discrepancy.
