Catalogue No. RO-QING-089

Coral-Red Ground Vase with Peonies, Birds, and Poetic Inscription — Qianlong Period (1736–1795), Attributed

Baluster-form vase with a gently swelling body, tall neck, and slightly flared mouth; overglaze famille rose enamel decoration of peonies and a perched bird on a saturated coral-red ground; a brush-written inscription in gold/olive enamel on the reverse; turquoise-glazed base with a small underglaze-blue seal-form mark. Attributed by Prof. N. Dronova to the Qianlong period on stylistic grounds.

Coral-Red Ground Famille Rose Peonies & Bird Poetic Inscription Underglaze-Blue Mark Qianlong Period (attributed)

Object Description

The vase has a gently swelling body, a tall neck, and a mouth with a slight outward flare, standing on a plain foot. The exterior is covered in a saturated coral-red enamel ground, against which the decoration is painted directly rather than reserved in a white panel.

The painted decoration shows peonies in white, pink, and purple tones with pale green and turquoise foliage, together with smaller sprays of pink blossom and a bird with pale yellow-green plumage and dark-marked wings, perched among the branches near the shoulder. The unglazed underside of the vase, visible at the base of the design, shows rockwork rendered in blue-grey tones.

Note
On the bird's wing and breast area (Fig. 3), a faint pearlescent/iridescent sheen is visible across part of the pale plumage, distinct from the surrounding matte-to-glossy enamel elsewhere on the vase. This is recorded here as an optical surface effect observed in the photograph; no claim is made about its cause (e.g., glaze composition, coating, or photographic lighting artefact).

Elsewhere on the red ground, a vertical column of characters is brush-written in a gold/olive-toned enamel, forming part of what appears to be a poetic inscription; individual characters are legible in the photograph (including forms resembling 曉, 蕾, 散, and 淺綠), but the full text and its authorship are not established here.

The base is glazed turquoise and bears a small, compact seal-form mark in underglaze blue, arranged in a square grid.

Attribution
Per the expert stylistic assessment of Prof. N. Dronova, D.Tech.Sci., author of this catalogue, this vase is attributed to the Qianlong period (1736–1795) on the basis of form, palette, and painting style. As the base mark is not legible enough to independently confirm a reading, this dating rests on connoisseurship judgment rather than on a verified reign mark.
Note
The accompanying documentation does not provide a specific reading for this mark. At the resolution supplied, the individual characters are not clearly legible as readable script. The mark is described here only as a compact underglaze-blue seal-form mark of undetermined text, considered together with the vessel's form and decoration rather than as a proof of date.

Optical Microscopy

The observation below concerns the coral-red ground enamel, examined by optical/macro photography. This section is limited to optical features; the elemental composition of the pigment and glaze is not addressed here.

Coral-Red Ground Enamel

Instrument Unconfirmed

The red enamel surface shows a fine, even granular texture with scattered small bright specks distributed fairly uniformly across the field, and a few faint darker patches. No hard-edged boundary or inclusion of note is visible within the frame. The imaging device and exact magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary and limited to optical appearance.
