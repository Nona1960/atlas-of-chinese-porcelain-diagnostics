Catalogue No. RO-QING-081

Hexagonal Sapphire-Blue Ground Vase with Koi Carp Decoration

Hexagonal vase with globular hexagonal-paneled body, waisted hexagonal neck, and spreading hexagonal foot; deep cobalt-blue underglaze ground on the body with overglaze iron-red koi carp outlined in gold amid gold-incised wave patterns; famille rose decoration on a turquoise ground on the neck and foot; two gilt scroll-form handles; a seal-form mark in underglaze blue on the turquoise-glazed base.

Cobalt-Blue Underglaze Ground Iron-Red Overglaze Enamel Gold Outlining Famille Rose on Turquoise Hexagonal Form Qing Dynasty

Object Description

The vase has a hexagonal, globular body rising to a waisted hexagonal neck and standing on a spreading hexagonal foot, with a meander (key-fret) border in iron red at the rim. The body carries a deep cobalt-blue underglaze ground beneath a thick, glossy transparent glaze. Two large koi carp are rendered in overglaze iron-red enamel, each scale individually outlined in gold, swimming among stylised wave patterns that are incised into the blue ground and picked out in gold line-work.

The hexagonal neck is decorated in famille rose enamels on a pale turquoise ground, with floral scrollwork and lotus-form motifs in a soft palette of pink, blue, yellow, and green, bordered above by a narrow iron-red meander band and below by a band of stylised lappets. The neck interior is glazed in a bright turquoise, visible in the top-down view of the mouth.

Two gilt handles are set at the neck-shoulder junction, formed as smooth curved scroll shapes.

Note
The accompanying documentation describes these as "lion-head handles" (獅子). In the supplied photographs, the handles read as curved gilt scroll forms; individual leonine features — eyes, mane, muzzle — are not clearly resolved at the available resolution and angle. The handles are described here as seen, without adopting the "lion-head" identification as confirmed.

The foot is similarly decorated with famille rose enamels on turquoise, echoing the neck. The base is glazed turquoise and bears a small squarish seal-form mark in underglaze blue, set within a double-square border.

Note
The accompanying documentation reads this as a four-character mark, 乾隆御製 (Qianlong yuzhi). The mark's division into a compact grid within the double square is consistent with a four-character arrangement, but individual strokes are not fully legible at the resolution supplied. The mark is recorded here per the accompanying documentation, and — as with any mark — it is considered together with the vessel's form, ground, and painting rather than as an isolated proof of date.
Scope Note
The accompanying documentation frames this object within an extended technical and historical discussion, including elemental-composition claims (e.g. XRF/Raman/pXRF findings, cobalt Mn/Fe ratios) and a proposed interpretive concept ("Sapphire Depth Effect") for the visual depth of the cobalt ground, along with a confident authentication verdict. Elemental analysis, compositional attribution, and authentication conclusions fall outside the scope of this catalogue entry, which is limited to what is directly observable in the supplied photographs and to the optical microscopy observations below. The visual depth and luminosity of the cobalt-blue ground are visible in Figs. 1–3 and can be described qualitatively, without adopting a diagnostic label or compositional explanation for the effect.

Optical Microscopy

The observations below concern the iron-red overglaze enamel and the fired-gold outlining, examined by optical/macro photography. This section is limited to optical features; the elemental composition of the pigments and glazes is not addressed here.

Iron-Red Enamel, Boundary Detail

Instrument Unconfirmed

A band of deep red enamel with a glossy, slightly uneven surface meets a granular, light-toned ground along a fairly sharp boundary; a bright specular highlight runs along part of the enamel surface. The imaging device and exact magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary and limited to optical appearance.

Iron-Red Enamel, Higher Magnification with Inclusions

Instrument Unconfirmed

The red enamel shows a fine, even granular texture scattered with small pale angular inclusions of varying size, along with occasional fine dark specks. A thin bright line crosses part of the frame, consistent with a surface scratch or fibre rather than a feature of the enamel itself. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.

Fired Gold, Directional Texture at Boundary

Instrument Unconfirmed

A diagonal band of golden-toned material shows a fine, directional, fibrous texture, meeting a paler, smoother glazed surface along an irregular boundary. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary and limited to optical appearance.

Iron-Red / Gold Transition Zone

Instrument Unconfirmed

A soft, diffusing transition is visible between a deep red zone and an adjacent golden-toned band, without a hard-edged boundary between the two colours. The image is somewhat soft/out of focus at this magnification. The imaging device and magnification are not documented, and the observation is presented as preliminary.
