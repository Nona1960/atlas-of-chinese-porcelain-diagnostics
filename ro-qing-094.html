Catalogue No. RO-QING-094

Archaistic Famille Verte Vase with Four Loop Handles

Vase with a tall cylindrical neck, rounded body, and applied striped loop handles at the shoulder; archaistic decorative programme in famille verte enamels (green, iron-red, cobalt blue, yellow) inspired by ancient ritual bronze ornament, including confronting dragon-form creatures, lotus-petal and lappet bands, and a lower band of geometric fret panels; white-glazed base with a legible underglaze-blue six-character reign mark.

Famille Verte Palette Archaistic Bronze-Form Decoration Striped Loop Handles Guangxu Reign Mark Qing Dynasty

Object Description

The vase has a tall, waisted cylindrical neck with a flared rim and a rounded, broad body. Two applied loop handles, painted with alternating stripes of red, green, yellow, and blue in imitation of a cord or ribbon, are visible at the shoulder in the supplied front view.

Note
The accompanying documentation describes four such loop handles, two of which would be on the reverse of the vase. No photograph of the reverse was supplied, so the presence of the additional two handles is recorded here per the documentation but was not independently confirmed by an image.

The neck is decorated in registers: a band of petal-form panels at the rim, a band of stylised red-and-green motifs below it, a narrow striped band, a band of pendant lappets, and a scalloped valance of shield-form panels in green, yellow, and red bordered in blue and red line-work. Below this, on the shoulder, a pair of confronting stylised creatures with open jaws, curling brows, and scrolled bodies is painted in pale green outlined in blue, flanking a small dot motif between them.

Note
The accompanying documentation identifies this shoulder motif as "taotie masks," a term usually referring to a frontal, symmetrical zoomorphic mask on ancient ritual bronzes. In the supplied photographs, the motif reads as a pair of confronting creatures shown in profile (each with a single visible eye, open jaw, and curling body) rather than a single frontal mask. This entry describes the motif as seen; the "taotie" identification is noted as the documentation's own iconographic reading rather than independently confirmed.

The lower body carries a band of angular, interlocking geometric panels in blue, red, and green, above a plain foot with a narrow yellow-and-blue border.

The base is glazed white and bears a clearly legible underglaze-blue mark in two vertical columns of three characters each, reading 大清光緒年製 (Da Qing Guangxu Nian Zhi, "Made in the Guangxu reign of the Great Qing"). This reading matches the accompanying documentation and is directly confirmed by the photograph.
